# PREREG — R-P1.28 confidence sharpness, and the adversarial case (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run (the checklist enforces it from 2026-11-23, but there is no reason to wait a
day). Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.28.

R-P1.27 swept all five peaked priors at mass **0.8** and concluded the hedge is
*dominated in expectation* (mean cost 0.60 vs the hedge's 1). That conclusion has
an obvious escape hatch which the sweep did not close: **a prior of mass 1.0 is
the naive planner**, and R-P1.24 measured the naive worst case at **1/4**. So the
world *does* contain an adversarial case — it is just not reachable at 0.8
confidence, because the residual weight rescues the choice.

This card closes the hatch: sweep the peak mass, and find where robustness starts
to pay.

## Design

R-P1.21/R-P1.24/R-P1.27 harness, unchanged: `o = (40,60,35,65)`,
`o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, tasks `x ∈ {42,46,48,52}` on lane `y = 45`, `t* = 90`,
drifts `v ∈ {−1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1}`, belief set `Δ = {−20,−10,0,+10,+20}`.

Five peak locations × three **masses** `p ∈ {0.8, 0.95, 1.0}` = **15 planners**,
plus minimax (cost 1) and the oracle (4/4). At `p = 1.0` the planner is exactly
the naive planner of R-P1.21, with the same smallest-`|v|` tie-break.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (the adversarial case exists, at full confidence).** At `p = 1.0` the costs
  are **{3, 3, 0, 2, 3}** for peaks `{−20, −10, 0, +10, +20}`: a fully confident
  wrong belief costs **3 of 4** task events.
- **P2 (mean cost rises with confidence).** `mean(0.8) = 0.60` < `mean(0.95)` <
  `mean(1.0) = 2.20`, and at least one peak's cost is strictly larger at 1.0 than
  at 0.8.
- **P3 (so robustness pays against certainty, not against mild error).** At
  `p = 1.0` the hedge's cost (1) is **below both the mean (2.20) and the maximum
  (3)**; at `p = 0.8` it was above the mean. R-P1.27's "dominated" is therefore a
  property of *0.8 confidence*, not of robustness — both statements stand, scoped.
- **P4 (the law underlies every plan).** For all 15 chosen drifts, predicted
  identifiability under the true geometry equals the measured count.

## Prior

**0.8** for P1/P4 (they follow from the tie-break rules, which are declared in
the code and were exercised in R-P1.21). **0.6** for P2's monotonicity: 0.95 is
nearly 1.0 but the residual 0.05 may still rescue some picks, and "nearly"
shifting a discrete choice is exactly where my last three predictions failed.

### The finding, sharper than predicted

> The cost of a wrong self-model is a **cliff at certainty, not a gradient in
> confidence**. At mass 0.95 five wrong beliefs cost at most 1 task event; at
> mass 1.00 the same beliefs cost 2–3. Any residual weight — even 5 % — rescues
> the choice, because the loss is a discrete ranking flip and a flip needs a pure
> prior.

So robustness (minimax) pays against an observer that is **certain and wrong**,
and is dominated by one that is merely **confident and wrong**. Both R-P1.27 and
this card stand; they describe different confidence regimes, and the boundary
between them is a step function, not a slope.

### Process consequence (systematic, not again)

Three cards in a row produced predictions by reading a plan table **by hand**, and
all three were wrong in the same way — a tie-break or a residual weight, not the
science. Preregs now generate their plan tables: `scripts/plan-table.py` prints
the predicted picks for a harness, so the numbers in a prereg are copied from an
artifact instead of transcribed.
