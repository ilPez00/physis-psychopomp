# Preregistration — R-P1.63: price one acquisition against one missed edge

Claim: registered at **prior 0.35**. Freeze on origin before any measurement.
Status at freeze: **not measured**.

Follows R-P1.62, which measured that no single probe clears the precision bar at
any sensor quality while budget alone reaches it only by probing the whole
candidate pool (21 of 21 at q=0.8). That card ended with an explicit stop: *"an
unpriced query is not a cost … the next honest card prices one acquisition
against one missed edge — not another sweep."* This is that card.

## The question

R-P1.62 showed the probe resolves edges but the *policy* never spends. Both
results are about accuracy at no stated price. A real operator does not ask
"can I reach 0.294 precision"; it asks **"is this query worth its price?"**

So: fix a price for one query in units of one missed edge, and ask whether any
policy's **net utility** beats proposing nothing.

## Utility, declared before measuring

```
U(policy) = V · correct  −  V · false_corrections  h  −  c · queries
```

- `V = 1.0` per edge: a correctly proposed edge earns one unit, a false proposal
  is charged `h = 1.0` (the R-P1.60 accounting: a wrong edge costs twice what it
  earns, because it must also be un-learned). This is fixed, not swept — sweeping
  both price and penalty would let any conclusion be reached by choosing axes.
- `c ∈ {2.0, 1.0, 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01}` — the price of one query in
  the same units. `c = 1.0` means one query costs exactly one missed edge.
- **Passive** proposes its top 3 and pays no query cost. **Scanner** probes all
  21. **Active** queries sequentially while its expected marginal utility is
  positive (`E[ΔU] > 0` under the current posterior), and stops.

`E[ΔU]` for a candidate probe is computed analytically from the posterior: for
each surviving world, the probe's reading under the declared sensor tells
whether the edge is present; the posterior after each reading is compared on
`V·correct − V·h·false` over the *policy's own top-3 after that update*, weighted
by the reading's marginal probability. A reading that cannot change the top-3
therefore has zero value and is not worth buying at any `c > 0`.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the stop rule bites.** At `c = 1.0`, the active policy buys **zero**
  queries on at least 10 of 12 held-out cases, and its net utility equals
  passive's. A query that costs a whole missed edge cannot be repaid by
  improving one of three proposal slots.
- **P2 — there is a price at which querying pays.** For some `c ≤ 0.2`, active
  net utility exceeds passive's on **at least 8 of 12** held-out cases. Predicted
  crossing: `c = 0.05`.
- **P3 — the optimal budget shrinks as price rises.** Mean queries bought at
  `c = 0.01` is **strictly greater** than at `c = 0.5` (pooled over held-out
  cases). Monotone non-increasing in `c` at every step.
- **P4 — buying everything is worse than buying wisely.** The scanner's net
  utility is strictly below the active policy's at every `c ≤ 0.5`, because 21
  queries at any positive price exceed what 11 additional correct edges can
  repay.
- **P5 — the earlier cards' accuracy result is a special case.** At `c = 0`
  (free queries) the active policy's precision matches R-P1.62's sequential
  `q=0.8, k=8` cell within the recorded draw spread (0.278, sd from 24 draws).

Any prediction may fail; a failure is a result and is kept.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say a priced query policy exists or does not exist at a declared
  price, and name the price at which it stops paying.
- It **may not** claim a learner, headroom on real data, or generalisation: this
  is the same synthetic generator, one predicate, one closure rule, one price
  axis. `E6` stays closed regardless of the verdict — a priced synthetic win is
  not evidence about a real instrument.
- It **may not** retune any earlier card's thresholds or receipts.
- If P2 fails, the honest conclusion is that **buying information about this
  fixture never pays at any price tested**, which is a stronger and more useful
  negative than R-P1.62's.

## Determinism and honesty checks

- Same generator and seeds as R-P1.61/R-P1.62 (`DEV, HELDOUT = 12, 12`), so this
  is a reanalysis of the same sample — declared as a limit, not an independent
  confirmation.
- Every reading comes from the simulated instrument, seeded per (case, query
  index). Labels reach the scorer only.
- Two negative controls, both must hold or the run is `UNVERIFIABLE`:
  a **permuted-truth** arm (readings generated against a shuffled truth) and an
  **uninformative** arm (readings independent of truth). Neither may beat passive
  net utility at any `c > 0`.
- A mutation check: disabling the `E[ΔU] > 0` stop rule must make the
  `c = 1.0` result worse, or the stop rule is not what the card claims.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`, never folded together.

## Limits

- One symmetric sensor (`TPR = 0.8 / FPR = 0.2`); R-P1.62 showed quality alone
  cannot clear the bar, so the price axis is measured at that quality only.
- Independent readings, perfectly local to one candidate edge.
- Utility assumes a fixed `V` and fixed `h`; a real deployment prices an edge
  differently per domain and no claim here transfers without re-measuring `h`.
