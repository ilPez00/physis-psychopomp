# Preregistration — R-P1.75: partially overlapping producers

Claim `a362d23e-06f7-4327-b129-966e15d664f5`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

D7's remaining clause. R-P1.46 gave both producers the **same** mask, so their
coverage was always identical or disjoint — "partially overlapping" was never a
case. R-P1.74 then showed that with full overlap a constant tag error is a
relabelling that distinguishability cannot see, and that the decision inverts.

This card asks the question those two leave open: **does partial overlap give a
rule something full overlap does not** — redundancy that survives one producer
being wrong?

## Coupling declared before the design

R-P1.46's `delivered(log, mask)` takes one mask for one marker. Partial overlap
needs **two markers and a per-producer view**, which the frozen signature cannot
express. So this card does **not** reuse that function: it builds a view over
R-P1.46's worlds with the same event shape and the same tag rule, and states the
coupling rather than implying comparability:

- the two worlds still differ only in a marker's **tag**, exactly as R-P1.46 built
  them;
- every producer still delivers at **half the mass per marker**, so "matched
  marginals" holds per marker as well as in aggregate;
- what changes is only **which markers a producer sees**.

Two markers, `m1` and `m2`, both present in both worlds. Per-producer views:

| arm | producer A sees | producer B sees | overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| full | `m1, m2` | `m1, m2` | both |
| disjoint | `m1` | `m2` | none |
| partial-A-wide | `m1, m2` | `m2` | `m2` |
| partial-cross | `m1` | `m1, m2` | `m1` |

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the anchor holds.** With perfect content, every arm distinguishes the two
  worlds (they differ in tag and at least one delivery occurs). Recorded as the
  control, not as a result: R-P1.46 already established that distinguishability is
  cheap.
- **P2 — partial overlap survives one wrong producer where full overlap does not.**
  Make producer A deliver the other world's tag, as R-P1.74's W1 did:

  - **full**: A is wrong on *both* markers, and since B sees the same two, every
    delivered tag is inverted. A majority over markers names the **wrong** world.
  - **partial-A-wide**: A is wrong on both, but B sees only `m2` and is correct
    there. So one marker is inverted and one is right, and a majority is a **tie**
    rather than an inversion.
  - **partial-cross**: A is wrong only on `m1`; B sees `m1` and `m2` and is correct
    on both. The correct marker outnumbers the inverted one, so a majority names
    the **right** world.

  Predicted: decided-world accuracy is `full = 0`, `partial-A-wide = 1/2 (tie)`,
  `partial-cross = 1`.
- **P3 — disjoint is uninformative, not wrong.** With no overlap, A's wrong `m1`
  and B's correct `m2` are the only evidence, so a majority ties. Predicted `1/2`.
- **P4 — the anonymous control ties everywhere.** Strip tags: every arm ties, as
  R-P1.46 and R-P1.74 both measured. A non-tie here would mean the tag is not the
  only payload.
- **P5 — marginals are matched per marker.** For each marker, each producer
  delivers it in exactly half the mass in every arm; the receipt asserts this
  rather than reporting it.

Any prediction may fail and is kept. **P2 is the load-bearing one**: if partial
overlap is no better than full under a wrong producer, then overlap buys nothing
and D7's "partially overlapping" clause has a negative answer worth having.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether partial overlap provides redundancy against a
  consistently wrong producer, and quantify it with exact fractions.
- It **may not** claim a fusion rule. This is majority-over-markers as a *scoring
  rule for the question*, not a proposed algorithm, and no decoder is evaluated.
- It **may not** claim comparability with R-P1.46's numbers: the fixture is
  rebuilt (two markers, per-producer views) and the coupling is stated above.
- `E6` stays closed.

## Controls and honesty

- Both worlds are R-P1.46's, unchanged in construction; only the marker count and
  the per-producer view are new.
- Per-producer-per-marker delivery mass is asserted, so "matched" cannot drift.
- P4's anonymous arm is the control that isolates the tag; P5's assertion is the
  control that isolates the marginal.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

Two markers, two producers, two worlds, one step, exact fractions. "Consistently
wrong" is a constant inversion, as in R-P1.74; correlated per-delivery errors and
more than two markers are not covered, and a tie is reported as a tie rather than
broken by a tie-rule.

### Two things I got wrong, both arithmetic I should have done before predicting

1. **`full` ties, it does not invert.** With A and B both seeing both markers and
   A wrong, each world's deliveries contain A's inverted tag twice and B's correct
   tag twice — a **tie**, not an inversion. R-P1.74's W2 inverted because it
   compared *delivered sequences between worlds*; this card counts tag votes
   *within* a world, and the two questions differ. Same lesson as last card, in the
   other direction: the metric decides the answer.
2. **`partial-A-wide` decides `wrong`.** A is wrong on `m1` *and* `m2`; B sees only
   `m2`. So the world's deliveries carry two wrong tags and one right one — wrong
   wins 2–1. A tie needs equal counts, and 2 vs 1 is not equal. My predicted "tie"
   assumed the overlap marker cancelled the extra one, which it does not.

### What the fixture does show

**Partial overlap helps exactly when the correct deliveries outnumber the
inverted ones**, and `partial-cross` is the arm that gets there: A is wrong only on
`m1`, while B covers `m1` and `m2` correctly, so right wins 2–1 and the decision
survives. That is the useful, narrow claim D7 asked for:

- full and disjoint overlap give a **tie** under one wrong producer;
- `partial-A-wide` gives the **wrong** answer, because the wrong producer covers
  strictly more markers;
- `partial-cross` gives the **right** answer.

So "partially overlapping" is not a property that helps on its own — **which side
covers more of the intersecting markers** is. A card claiming overlap as a
mitigation would have been wrong, and this one says so instead.

### What this closes, and what it does not

- **Closes D7**: incorrect tags (R-P1.74) and partial overlap (this card) are both
  measured, with the anonymous and matched-marginal controls D7 required.
- **Does not claim a fusion rule.** Majority-over-markers is the *scoring rule for
  the question*, not a proposed algorithm, and no decoder was evaluated.
- **Not comparable to R-P1.46's fractions** — the view is rebuilt for two markers,
  as the card declared before the run.
- A one-line correction to my own first run is also recorded: `decide()` initially
  scored each world against its own tag on both sides, so every arm tied including
  the perfect one, which was a scoring bug rather than a finding.

`E6` stays closed.
