# Preregistration — R-P1.49: organizational identity under complete turnover
(2026-11-23)

Claim `297b0fac-4996-4e71-b9c0-bff55314a966`. Prior: **0.85**. Freeze and verify
this file on origin before implementing or running the card. Status at freeze:
**not measured**.

This is C1 in the [P1 plan](../tasks/plan.md). It tests one operational version of
"identity is the constraints a system preserves while its parts change": replace
**every** initial component, disturb the organization, and ask whether the same
organization comes back. It is a discrete toy, not a biological claim, and it
cannot show that any particular individual persists — see **Limits**.

## System

Two roles, integer loads `x = (x1, x2)`, `x ∈ {0..8}²`. Invariant set
`I = {x : |x1 − x2| ≤ 1}`. Transfers are internal: a transfer moves one unit from
one role to the other and conserves `x1 + x2`. Everything else is *not* a transfer.

Two arms share one nominal trajectory, one component inventory, one replacement
schedule and one perturbation:

- **feedback** — at each tick, if `|x1 − x2| ≥ 2`, transfer 1 from the higher to
  the lower role (ties: lower index first). Otherwise do nothing.
- **open-loop** — replay the *nominal* transfer sequence for that tick, recorded
  from the feedback arm's undisturbed run. On the nominal trajectory that sequence
  is empty, so open-loop performs no transfers ever.

**Replacement.** Component identities `c1, c2` (one per role) are replaced by fresh
ones at ticks 1 and 2 respectively, each swap preserving its role's load and type,
so the inventory is *new* while the role structure is unchanged. No initial
component may be present after tick 2: the run asserts `replaced ⊇ {c1, c2}`.

**Perturbation.** At tick 3, `p` units are added to role `t` from outside the
system (`p ∈ {0, 1, 2}`, `t ∈ {1, 2}`). This breaks conservation on purpose: the
invariant is about balance, not about total.

Observe ticks 4–6 (three observations after the disturbance).

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **O1 — capacity.** Every run replaces **100 %** of initial components and the
  replacement log names both swaps. The undisturbed (`p = 0`) observable
  trajectories of the two arms are **identical**, so nothing observable separates
  the arms before the disturbance.
- **O2 — the discriminating cell.** At `p = 2`, on **both** targets: feedback is
  inside `I` at tick 4 and stays inside for ticks 4, 5, 6 (residence 3/3);
  open-loop is outside `I` at tick 4 and remains outside through tick 6
  (residence 0/3). Reported per cell, not pooled.
- **O3 — where it must abstain.** At `p = 1` and at the sham `p = 0`, **both** arms
  are inside `I` from tick 4 onwards (residence 3/3 each). The card claims **no**
  discrimination there and records an equivalence, not a win.
- **O4 — the verdict is structural, not nominal.** For `p = 2`: permuting role ids
  leaves every verdict unchanged; and switching the arm to open-loop **while
  keeping the identical inventory and replacement log** removes recovery on both
  targets. If either fails, O4 is falsified and O2 may not be quoted alone.

Ties count as failure for O2 (a tie cannot separate the arms), and success for O3
(a tie is what is predicted). No cell may be dropped, retuned, or re-run to a
different outcome; the run prints every cell.

## Limits — stated, not hidden

- This shows organizational continuity is **discriminable under these
  interventions**. Two independent copies started at `(4, 4)` with the same rule
  would pass the same test, so nothing here distinguishes one continuing individual
  from two alike ones. A causal-lineage requirement would be a separate card.
- The feedback rule is declared, not learned; "self-maintenance" here means the
  declared rule restores the declared invariant.
- One perturbation magnitude, one invariant, two roles. No noise, no partial
  turnover, no continuous time.
- Replacement is modelled by identity change with load preserved. A world where
  replacement also perturbs the load is a different card.
