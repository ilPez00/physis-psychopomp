# Preregistration — R-P1.65: a rotating observer, where coverage is the question

Claim `23576a80-0d32-4939-8ec3-ec3585dcc64f`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

The last untouched axis in the mission brief (§3 D), and the one R-P1.18 left
open: `scene_symmetry` proved identifiability covariant under **reflections and
translations** of the whole scene, not under rotation.

## The question, and why it is not "does rotation work"

`transform_observable` already rotates positions and velocities (constant
`theta`, drifting origin), and R-P1.2/R-P1.3 pinned the round trip and the
no-leak property. So rotation is not untested code.

**What is untested is whether an occluder keeps covering the same events when
the observer rotates.** A blind region is an axis-aligned rectangle; rotating
the frame maps it to a rotated rectangle, and a rotated rectangle is not one of
the shapes this simulator can express. So there are two candidate facts and the
card must separate them:

- **F1 — the transform is exact.** Rotating an episode and inverting it returns
  the original observables within tolerance, and the invariants (world count,
  truth-in-worlds) are unchanged. Expected to hold; a failure here is a bug.
- **F2 — the *decision* is covariant only if the occluder rotates with the
  world.** Under a bodies-only rotation (occluder left axis-aligned) the blind
  region no longer covers the same point, so identifiability legitimately
  changes. That is R-P1.18's bodies-only control result, restated for rotation.

If F1 holds and F2 holds, the honest conclusion is that **rotation is not a
symmetry of this world model**: it is a coordinate change for the bodies and not
for the occluders, and "is the event covered" is therefore not rotation-invariant
unless the scene rotates as a whole — which the axis-aligned occluder cannot
represent without a polygon.

## Fixture

R-P1.9's geometry (one pair, `HIDDEN_KICK` at t=60, observable log only), the
same `INIT` and occluder as `tests/test_multi_observer.py`. Rotations
`theta ∈ {0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2}`. Two arms per rotation:

- **scene**: the observation is transformed *and* the query point is transformed
  by the same inverse — the fair comparison, testing whether the decision moves
  with the coordinates.
- **bodies-only**: the observation is transformed, the query point is not — the
  control that must change.

5 seeds each. Blind-region coverage computed from the occluder and the
transformed event position, printed in the receipt, never assumed.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the transform round-trips.** For every rotation, transforming and then
  inverting the observable log reproduces it within `1e-9`, and `|v|` is
  preserved per event. This is the mission's own invertibility test (§6).
- **P2 — the world count does not move.** `consistent_worlds` returns the same
  count and the truth survives in **5/5 seeds at every rotation** in the scene
  arm. Rotation translates coordinates; it must not create or destroy worlds.
- **P3 — the decision follows the query point, not the numbers.** In the scene
  arm the identify/tie verdict is **unchanged from `theta = 0`** at every
  rotation, 5/5 seeds. Predicted to hold for all five rotations.
- **P4 — the bodies-only arm changes.** At least one rotation must flip the
  verdict relative to `theta = 0`, because the axis-aligned occluder stops
  covering the (unrotated) event once the bodies rotate away from it. A
  bodies-only arm that never changes would mean the transform silently moved the
  occluder too — the leak this card is looking for.
- **P5 — no oracle leak.** The transformed log carries no field naming the
  hidden state: no `truth`, no joint-row index, no `S`, and the frame's `theta`
  is not in provenance. Checked by field-name scan, not by trust.

Any prediction may fail; a failure is a result and is kept. P4 failing is the
most informative outcome and would block any claim of rotation covariance.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether rotation is a symmetry of this model, and separate "the
  arithmetic is right" from "the decision is invariant".
- It **may not** claim a rotated *observer model*: the occluder here is a fixed
  rectangle in world coordinates, and a real rotating camera would sweep its
  blind region across the scene over time — R-P1.17's time-resolved coverage, not
  this card.
- It **may not** relabel an axis-aligned limitation as a property of rotation in
  general.
- `E6` stays closed: nothing here licenses a learner.

## Controls and honesty

- The `theta = 0` arm is the identity control and must reproduce R-P1.9 exactly
  (unique with the two observers, tie with one) or the fixture is wrong.
- Tolerance is declared, not tuned: `1e-9` for round-trip, and verdicts are
  compared as categories, never as distances.
- Every cell prints its occluder, event position and coverage flag so P4 is
  checkable rather than narrated.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

- Five rotations, one geometry, one kick, five seeds.
- A rotated rectangle is approximated by nothing here: the card does not rotate
  occluders, and says so. Extending the simulator to polygon occluders is a
  separate change with its own gates.
