# Preregistration — R-P1.62: is the probe's quality or its budget the constraint?

Claim `8c130cc6-e95d-45b4-b45c-81993bbf4d8e`. Prior **0.5**.
Freeze on origin before the sweep runs. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

Follows R-P1.61, which bought **one** probe at `TPR 0.8 / FPR 0.2`, measured
`NO HEADROOM`, and concluded: *"the sensor, not the policy, is the binding
constraint."* This card exists to test that conclusion, because hand arithmetic
on the frozen fixture says it is wrong.

## The arithmetic that motivates the card

A candidate edge's prior is `3 / 21.5 ≈ 0.14`. A symmetric sensor with
`TPR = 1 − FPR = q` moves that edge to

```
present:  q·π / (q·π + (1−q)(1−π))
absent :  (1−q)π / ((1−q)π + q(1−π))
```

At `q = 0.99` a `present` reading puts the probed edge at **0.94**, and the
policy still fills its other two proposal slots with guesses. Expected precision:

```
E[P] ≈ P(present) · (0.94 + 2·(2/20)) / 3  +  P(absent) · (3/21)
     ≈ 0.147 · 0.380 + 0.853 · 0.143 ≈ 0.175
```

R-P1.61's held-out bar was `B_precision + 0.10 = 0.294`. **0.175 < 0.294, with a
near-perfect sensor.** So raising `q` cannot clear the bar, and "the sensor is
the binding constraint" is not supported: one probe resolves **one of the three
missing edges** at any quality. The budget is what binds.

## What is measured

A sweep over the frozen R-P1.61 machinery — same generator, same seeds, same
candidate pools, same arms — varying two declared parameters:

- `q ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, 1.0}`, symmetric
  (`TPR = q`, `FPR = 1 − q`), one query.
- `k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 21}`, queries on the `k` edges with highest
  expected information gain (ties by edge key), at `q = 0.8` (the R-P1.61
  sensor) and at `q = 1.0`.

Readings independent per query, seeded deterministically. Posterior is uniform
over surviving worlds, filtered by every reading. Proposals: top 3 by posterior
mass. Truth is read by the scorer and the simulated instrument only.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — quality alone never clears the bar.** For every `q` at `k = 1`, held-out
  top-3 precision is below `B_precision + 0.10`, including `q = 1.0`. Predicted
  `q = 1.0` value: **0.18 ± 0.05**.
- **P2 — precision increases monotonically in `q`.** Every step from `0.5` to
  `1.0` at `k = 1` is non-decreasing, pooled over 24 cases.
- **P3 — budget is the lever.** At `q = 0.8`, precision crosses
  `B_precision + 0.10` at some `k ≤ 8`, and at `q = 1.0` at some `k ≤ 4`.
- **P4 — completion error follows precision, not query count.** At the smallest
  `k` that crosses the precision bar, completion error is also below the passive
  arm's. A crossing that leaves completion error at or above baseline is not
  headroom and H5 of R-P1.61 keeps E6 closed.
- **P5 — the information account explains both.** `log2(C(21,3)) ≈ 10.4` bits are
  needed to name the three missing edges; one binary reading at `q` carries
  `1 − H₂(1−q)` bits. Recorded as arithmetic, not as a measurement.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** retract R-P1.61's sentence "the sensor, not the policy, is the
  binding constraint", and replace it with a budget statement, if P1 holds.
- It **may not** declare headroom or open the learner: a `k`-probe policy that
  clears the bar costs `k` queries, and no card has yet priced a query against a
  real acquisition cost. Headroom at `k = 21` is "buy the whole candidate set",
  which is not a research instrument, it is a scanner.
- No threshold here is retuned into R-P1.61's receipt. That card stays as
  measured.

## Limits

- Synthetic graphs, one predicate, one closure rule, one observation budget,
  one symmetric sensor family. Asymmetric `TPR/FPR` is out of scope.
- Every reading is assumed independent and perfectly local to one candidate edge.
  A real instrument that reports on many edges at once would break both
  assumptions and the information account with them.
- The sweep reuses seeds R-P1.61 already used, so it is a reanalysis of the same
  sample, not an independent confirmation.
