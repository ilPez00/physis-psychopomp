# PREREG — R-P1.19 does the rule survive rich physics? (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**, and the claim is registered in the physis
store before the run (the process miss from R-P1.18). Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md`
R-P1.19.

Every measurement so far used constant-velocity bodies on separated lanes. This
card adds the two things that could break a coverage rule: **gravity** (curved
trajectories, so the event position is no longer `v·t`) and **multi-contact**
(bodies that collide, several times, including collisions inside the blind
region — events that are unobservable in themselves but change what is seen
later).

## Instrument change

`SimConfig.accel: Vec = (0.0, 0.0)` — constant acceleration applied to velocity
each step (semi-implicit Euler), default zero, so every prior number is
unchanged. Collisions already exist and are already elastic equal-mass; nothing
else changes.

## Prediction rule (declared)

Per cell: run the world **without** a kick, read the body's position at the
event step, and predict `tie` iff that point is inside the blind region
`o ∩ o2(t)`; `unique` otherwise. This uses the world's own deterministic
trajectory — not the attribution outcome — so it is a prediction of
identifiability from geometry and dynamics, not a restatement of the result.

## Grid

Scenario A — **separated lanes** (`y = 45, 51`), scenario B — **same lane,
head-on** (`y = 45, 45`, ball 0 at `x0`, ball 1 at `x0+40`, velocities
`(±3, 0)`), which collides and can collide repeatedly after wall bounces.

Gravity: `(0, 0)` or `(0, −0.5)`. Event times: `t ∈ {30, 60, 90}`. Objective:
`o2 = (40.5, 60.5, 35, 65)` static, `steps = 200`, `dv = (0, 1.5)`.
2 × 2 × 3 = **12 cells**.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (rule holds in rich physics).** 12/12 cells match the predicted
  tie/unique. *One mismatch falsifies.*
- **P2 (the closed form is not enough).** With gravity, `x(t) = x0 + v·t` no
  longer predicts the event position: at least one gravity cell's closed-form
  prediction differs from the integrated one by more than the 1-unit lane gap.
  *Falsified if* the closed form matches every gravity cell — that would mean
  gravity is too weak to matter and the physics is not actually richer.
- **P3 (hidden contacts do not break it).** Cells containing at least one
  `ball_collide` event with `observable = False` still obey P1, and at least one
  such cell exists. *Falsified if* no hidden-collision cell exists, or one of
  them mismatches.
- **P4 (multi-contact does not break it).** At least one cell has ≥ 2 collisions
  and obeys P1; cells with ≥ 2 collisions show the same tie/unique behaviour as
  single-contact cells with the same coverage status.
  *Falsified if* no multi-contact cell exists, or any multi-contact cell
  mismatches.

## Prior

**0.55.** The rule is a per-event geometric predicate and the card is designed
to be able to falsify it: a kick whose effect is absorbed by a collision shortly
after, or a hidden collision that makes two hypotheses produce the same
observables, are both plausible ways for rich physics to break a coverage rule.
I expect the rule to hold for coverage but would not be surprised by a
falsification in the same-lane cells.

## Rules

- No retune. A mismatch is a receipt and names which mechanism broke it.
- 12 cells is the declared grid; no cell added afterwards.

## AMENDMENT 1 — 2026-11-22, before any verdict

Scenario B as written is **invalid**: `ball 1 at x0 + 40` with `x0 = 20` puts it
at `x = 60`, inside the first occluder, so `run()` refuses to build the episode.
Corrected construction, hypotheses and thresholds untouched:

- ball 0 at `x = 15`, `v = (+3, 0)`; ball 1 at `x = 85`, `v = (−9, 0)`, both on
  lane `y = 45`. Both start outside `o`; they meet inside the blind region
  (a **hidden** collision, which is what P3 needs), and the −9 speed makes a
  second contact reachable inside 200 steps (P4), which ±3 could not.

Six cells (`lanes`, both gravities, three times) had already run and matched
their predictions when this was corrected; none of them is affected by the
change and nothing from them is reused as evidence for scenario B.

- **P1 UPHELD** — 12/12 cells match the geometric prediction, so the rule
  survives curved trajectories and a collision.
- **P2 UPHELD, strongly** — under gravity the closed-form `x0 + v·t` is wrong by
  up to **39.8 units** (the body at `t = 90` is at `y = 24.5`, not `y = 45`, and
  `x` diverges after the collision), so the physics really is richer and the
  prediction had to come from the world's own trajectory.
- **P3 FALSIFIED — as a design failure, not as evidence about the rule.** Zero
  hidden-collision cells: the head-on pair met at `x ≈ 32.5`, *outside* the
  blind region `x ≥ 40.5`. My amendment claimed they met inside it; they do not.
- **P4 FALSIFIED, same reason**: zero cells with ≥ 2 collisions. With equal
  masses a head-on exchange separates the pair at equal speeds, and neither can
  re-contact the other inside 200 steps without a wall in between.

Both falsifications say **"this grid could not test the claim"**, not "the claim
is false". The distinction is recorded rather than blurred: R-P1.20 is built to
produce those events, and it **verifies the capacity of each cell before
running** — a cell that does not contain the required event class is reported as
not-run, never as a result.

### On the generalisation that P1 does support

A kick whose effect must travel through a later collision is exactly the case
where a coverage rule could plausibly fail — the perturbation is absorbed and
re-emitted — and it did **not** fail: the six head-on cells obey the geometric
prediction at every event time. That is a real (if narrow) strengthening.
