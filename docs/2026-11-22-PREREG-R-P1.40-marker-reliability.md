# PREREG — R-P1.40 identity identifiability = 1 − (1−p)^N (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.40.

R-P1.39 measured identity as a coverage question with **perfect** markers. The gap
it left is the statistical one: if each marker fires only sometimes, when does
identity become decidable? That has a closed form, which makes it a test rather
than a story.

## Claim under test

> Identity is resolvable iff **at least one** marker was observed. With `N` visible
> marker opportunities and per-opportunity reliability `p`, the probability is
> `1 − (1−p)^N` — so **coverage sets `N` and reliability sets `p`, and the two
> compose multiplicatively in the exponent.**

## Instrument

`SimConfig.marker_reliability: float = 1.0` — each marker opportunity fires with
probability `p`, drawn from its own deterministic stream (`marker_rng`, seeded from
seed/episode plus the stream index, the same discipline as the sensor-noise
streams). `1.0` reproduces R-P1.39 exactly.

## Design

World as R-P1.39 case 2 (`o = (40,60,35,65)`, `o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, two bodies
crossing the open box), relabelled counterpart compared as before.

- `p ∈ {1.0, 0.9, 0.7, 0.5, 0.2, 0.0}`, **200 realizations** each (episode index
  varied so the reliability draws differ);
- `N` measured at `p = 1.0` for the same geometry: count of markers emitted;
- a second geometry with a **shorter visible window** (bodies start closer to the
  blind region) to separate `p·N` from either factor alone.

Metric: fraction of realizations whose relabelled logs **differ** (identity
resolvable).

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **R1 (the formula).** For each `p`, the measured resolvable fraction is within
  **±0.05** of `1 − (1−p)^N` with `N` from the same geometry. With 200
  realizations the binomial standard error is ≤ 0.035, so this is a real test, not
  a tolerance for slop.
- **R2 (the floor).** At `p = 0` the resolvable fraction is **exactly 0** — the
  R-P1.39 M1 case recovered as the degenerate end of the formula.
- **R3 (coverage still gates it).** In the hidden geometry of R-P1.39 case 3
  (`N = 0`), the fraction is **0 at every p**, including `p = 1.0`: reliability
  cannot buy evidence that coverage never offered.
- **R4 (it is the product, not either factor).** The short-window geometry has a
  smaller `N`; at the `p` where the two geometries' predicted fractions differ by
  most, the measured difference is within **±0.10** of the predicted difference —
  i.e. the exponent, not `p` or `N` alone, is what predicts identifiability.

## Prior

**0.85** for R1/R2 (independence and the Bernoulli algebra are exact; the only risk
is an implementation detail of which steps count as opportunities). **0.9** for R3.
**0.6** for R4: separating the product from either factor needs the two windows to
differ enough, and my choice of "shorter window" is a guess made before measuring.

## Rules

- No retune. A falsified prediction is a receipt.
- `p` grid, 200 realizations, both geometries and the tolerance are declared here.
- Reliability is drawn per opportunity, independently: no bursty channel, which is
  a different (and harder) model not claimed here.

R1–R3 "held" and R4 "held" with a predicted gap of **0.000**. That is not a test:
with `N = 56`, `1 − (1−p)^56 ≈ 1` for every `p ≥ 0.2`, so the formula could not be
wrong; R2 is the only non-degenerate point (it is the algebra at `p = 0`); and my
"short-window" geometry produced the **same** `N = 56`, so R4 compared a cell with
itself.

**Recorded as vacuous, not as evidence.** The corrected design is R-P1.41, with `N`
controlled directly. The one durable product of this card is the instrument:
`SimConfig.marker_reliability` with its own deterministic stream.
