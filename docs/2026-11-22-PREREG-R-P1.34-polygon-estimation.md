# PREREG — R-P1.34 polygon estimation and where optimism comes from (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.34.

R-P1.33 left one thing measurable and one thing unexplained: polygon crossings
carried no edge label (now fixed — `side = "poly<edge>"` plus `normal`), and the
rect estimator's **optimism** was never tested off rectangles. This card does both.

## The question behind it

A rectangle edge gives a constraint of the form `x ≥ x0`; taking the *tightest*
bound from a crossing puts the estimate **inside** the true region — that is
R-P1.29's optimism. A polygon edge gives `n·x ≤ d`; taking the tightest bound
there puts the estimate **outside** — pessimism, from the same rule.

So the hypothesis is that optimism is not a property of rectangles at all, but of
**which side of the bound you take**: an estimator that always chooses the inner
side is optimistic for every geometry.

## Instrument

`estimate_polygon_halfplanes(episodes, step, inner: bool)` — per polygon edge,
using the labelled crossings:

- `raw` (`inner=False`): `d = min(n·p)` over crossings of that edge — the tightest
  bound in the **outside** direction;
- `inner` (`inner=True`): `d = min(n·p) − step·|n·v̂|`, subtracting the one-step
  penetration along the normal — the tightest bound in the **inside** direction.

## World

R-P1.33's: `o = (40,60,35,65)`, `o2 = (30,92,20,90)`, diamond
`(75,42),(83,50),(75,58),(67,50)`; corner task points `(69,44), (81,44), (69,56),
(81,56)` (outside the diamond, inside its bounding box) and the centre `(75,50)`

Corpus: eight episodes — horizontal movers on lanes `44, 50, 56` from both sides,
vertical movers at `x = 75` from both sides, plus two diagonal-ish lanes to reach
the remaining edges — enough to cross **all four** edges.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1.** All **four** edges are observed, and each offset is within **one step**
  of its truth under both rules.
- **P2 (the raw rule is pessimistic on polygons).** For every observed edge,
  `d_raw ≥ d_true`: the same "tightest bound" rule that produced optimism on
  rectangles produces pessimism here, because the constraint form flipped.
  *Falsified if* any edge under-covers under `raw`.
- **P3 (the inner rule is optimistic).** For every observed edge,
  `d_inner ≤ d_true + 1e-9`: choosing the inner side is optimistic **regardless of
  geometry**, which is the transferable claim.
- **P4 (and it is what matters for predictions).** Using the inner rule, the
  estimated polygon predicts identifiability correctly on the **centre and all
  four corners** (5/5); the raw rule calls at least one corner blind (the
  pessimism has consequences).

### The finding

> Optimism is not a property of rectangles. It is a property of **which side of
> the bound you take**. The same "tightest bound" rule that makes a rectangle
> estimate conservative (inside) makes a polygon estimate pessimistic (outside),
> because `x ≥ x0` and `n·x ≤ d` push in opposite directions. An estimator that
> always takes the inner side is optimistic for every occluder shape.

That explains R-P1.29–R-P1.32 in one sentence and tells you how to build a
self-model that errs safely: **always take the inner bound**, whatever the shape.

### One thing this corpus did not show

The raw rule's error was **0.00** on every edge — the crossings landed exactly on
the boundary vertices, so the pessimism had no consequence for the 5/5 prediction
check. The contrast P4 implied (raw calling a corner blind) therefore did **not**
appear; what the card establishes is the **signs** (P2/P3) and the inner rule's
accuracy, not a demonstrated cost of the raw rule in this geometry. A corpus whose
crossings land inside the step would show it, and is not claimed here.
