# PREREG — R-P1.30 end-to-end: calibrate, then plan (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.30.

The arc so far: a wrong self-model costs up to 3 of 4 task events (R-P1.21), the
cost is a cliff at certainty (R-P1.28), and the self-model is **recoverable** from
the observer's own log to one step, conservatively (R-P1.29). This card joins them
and asks the only question that is left:

> Does an agent that measures its own blind spot from its own log, and then plans
> with that measurement, actually do better than an agent that plans from a fixed
> belief — and does calibration make hedging unnecessary?

## Design

World and harness as R-P1.21/R-P1.24: `o = (40,60,35,65)`,
`o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, task events `x ∈ {42,46,48,52}` on lane `y = 45`,
`t* = 90`, one body per task, drifts `v ∈ {−1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1}`.

Four agents, all choosing one drift:

1. **uncalibrated-wrong** — believes its occluder sits at `o2 − 10` and plans from
   that belief (R-P1.21's worst naive plan: drift `−0.5`, measured **1/4**).
2. **calibrated** — runs `estimate_blind_region` on a corpus of its own logs (the
   R-P1.29 corpora: horizontal lanes and vertical columns), then predicts, for each
   candidate drift, which tasks would be visible by **translating its estimated
   blind rect** by `v·dt·t*`, and picks the best.
3. **calibrated-hedged** — same estimate, then a minimax over the *estimate's own*
   uncertainty: the three rects obtained by shrinking, keeping, and expanding the
   estimate by one step on every edge. Chooses the drift with the best worst case
   over those three.
4. **oracle** — plans from the true geometry (upper bound).

Measured the same way as every planning card: run the chosen drift, score each
task event by counterfactual attribution.

## Predictions (fixed now; plan table generated with `scripts/plan-table.py`)

- **P1 (calibration recovers the optimum).** `calibrated` picks a drift scoring
  **4/4**, i.e. equal to the oracle. The estimate is within one step and the
  optimal drift's margins are ≥ 2 units, so the ranking cannot flip.
- **P2 (calibration is worth 3 task events).** `calibrated` scores **4/4** against
  `uncalibrated-wrong`'s **1/4** — the same three-event gap R-P1.21 measured, now
  closed by the agent's own data.
- **P3 (calibration replaces hedging).** `calibrated-hedged` picks the **same**
  drift as `calibrated`. Once the observer's uncertainty is one step instead of
  ±20, the conservatism that R-P1.24 needed is no longer worth its price — the
  hedge collapses onto the calibrated choice.
- **P4 (the law underlies the plan).** Predicted identifiability from the **true**
  geometry equals the measured count for the calibrated plan.

## Prior

**0.8** for P1/P2/P4: the estimate error (0.3) is an order of magnitude below the
margins (≥ 2), so the ranking should be robust. **0.6** for P3: the hedged and
calibrated picks may differ if the one-step shrink/expand changes a predicted
count at a task point that sits close to an edge — `46` sits exactly on the blind
edge in this harness, which is where the estimates are least comfortable.

## Rules

- No retune. A falsified prediction is a receipt.
- The calibration corpus and the drift grid are declared here; no additional
  corpus afterward.
- The estimator is used exactly as shipped in `psychopomp/selfmodel.py` — the card
  measures the module, not a tuned variant.

### The finding, end to end

> An agent that measures its own blind region from its own log — to one step,
> conservatively — plans as well as one that is told the truth, and no longer
> needs the hedge that an uncalibrated agent does. **Calibration replaces
> conservatism**: the ±20 hedge of R-P1.24 paid because the model was wrong by up
> to 20 units; measuring reduces the error to 0.3 and the hedge becomes
> vestigial.

That is the whole arc in one sentence: coverage decides identifiability
(R-P1.12–R-P1.20), a wrong self-model costs up to three of four events
(R-P1.21), the cost is a cliff at certainty (R-P1.28), and the self-model is
learnable from the log (R-P1.29) — so the loop closes at the oracle (this card).

### Limits

One world, one corpus, one drift grid, noise-free. The "hedge" here is over the
estimator's one-step uncertainty, not a Bayesian posterior, and the corpus is
generated by the same simulator — a real camera would face occlusion of the
*edges* themselves (a body crossing outside the visible band) which this card does
not model.
