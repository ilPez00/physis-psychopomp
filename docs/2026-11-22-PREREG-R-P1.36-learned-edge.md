# PREREG — R-P1.36 the first licensed learned arm: a learned edge correction (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.36.

Every learned arm in this program was forbidden until now, and correctly: mission
P1 §4 licenses one only where the deterministic arms leave headroom. R-P1.35
measured that headroom — at σ = 1.0 the deterministic self-model estimator is
**pessimistic in 14/20 realizations** (11/20 even with a quantile bound), so it is
not at ceiling. This is the first card that may train anything.

## What is learned, exactly

Not the law, and not the geometry. A **correction to the edge estimate**:

- **input** (observable only): for one blind-region edge, the summary of the
  crossing bounds the observer actually recorded — min, 10/25/50/75/90 % quantiles,
  max, mean, standard deviation, sample count — plus the sensor noise level σ it
  knows it is running at;
- **output**: a scalar correction added to the inner-bound estimate;
- **target** (training only): the true edge minus that estimate.

The model never sees the true edge as an input, and the law is never replaced — the
estimate it corrects is the one R-P1.34 showed is optimistic under exact evidence.

## Data and split

Geometry from R-P1.29: `o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, blind `(46,54,35,65)`, step 0.3;
corpora of eight sweeps (five horizontal lanes, three vertical columns), one mover
per episode with a parked partner.

- **train**: σ ∈ {0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0}, **200 corpora**;
- **control**: identical architecture and data, **targets shuffled** across
  samples (a model that learns nothing real should reproduce the baseline);
- **held-out**: 20 fresh corpora at each of σ ∈ {0.3, 1.0, **1.5**} — 1.5 is
  outside the training range.

Architecture: 2-layer MLP (12 → 24 → 1, ReLU), Adam, 400 epochs, CPU, fixed seeds.
Four edges × 200 corpora = 800 training rows.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **L1 (it beats the deterministic estimator where the headroom is).** At held-out
  σ = 1.0 the learned correction's **pessimism rate is strictly lower** than the
  deterministic inner bound's.
- **L2 (it beats its own control).** At σ = 1.0 the learned model's pessimism rate
  is strictly lower than the shuffled-target model's, and the shuffled model is
  within 0.10 of the deterministic baseline (it should learn nothing).
- **L3 (it generalises off the training range).** At the unseen σ = 1.5 the learned
  correction's pessimism rate is also strictly below the deterministic one's.
  *Falsified if* the model only works inside its training σ range.
- **L4 (no accuracy regression).** On every held-out σ, mean absolute edge error is
  no worse than the deterministic estimator's by more than **0.05** (one sixth of a
  step).

## Prior

**0.55.** The headroom is real but narrow (14/20 → the ceiling for improvement is
the tail), the target is a per-edge scalar from noisy quantiles, and a small MLP on
800 rows can easily learn the noise scale and nothing else. I rate L1 at 0.6, L2 at
0.75, L3 at 0.45 (unseen σ is exactly where a fitted correction breaks), L4 at 0.7.

## Rules

- No retune. A falsified prediction is a receipt.
- The split, σ grid, architecture and epoch count are declared here.
- **A learned arm that does not beat both the deterministic baseline and its
  shuffled control is reported as a negative and no further training is done.**
  This is the trap the program's standing evidence says was fallen into before
  (Phase 11D: "toy win was a supervision confound").

## Execution status — run 1, 2026-11-22: L1/L2/L3 FALSIFIED, L4 upheld

| held-out σ | deterministic pessimistic | **learned** | shuffled control | baseline MAE | learned MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | **0.09** | 0.30 | 0.29 | 0.145 | **0.091** |
| 1.0 | **0.53** | 0.61 | 0.65 | 0.198 | 0.201 |
| 1.5 (unseen) | **0.61** | 0.68 | 0.74 | 0.302 | 0.307 |

- **L1 FALSIFIED** — at σ = 1.0 the learned correction is *worse* than the
  deterministic inner bound (0.61 vs **0.53** pessimism).
- **L2 FALSIFIED** — it does not beat its own shuffled control either (0.61 vs
  0.65): the difference is noise, not learning. This is the Phase 11D pattern —
  a "win" that a control also gets — and it is why the control exists.
- **L3 FALSIFIED** — no transfer: at the unseen σ = 1.5 it is worse (0.68 vs 0.61).
- **L4 UPHELD** — mean absolute error is not worse (and at σ = 0.3 it is **37 %
  better**: 0.145 → 0.091). That is the trap in one table.

### The finding, and it is the useful kind

> The learned model optimised the wrong quantity. It **halved the average error**
> at low noise while **tripling the pessimism rate** (0.09 → 0.30). For a
> self-model whose safety property is "never claim to have seen what you did not",
> average accuracy is the wrong objective: a squared-error loss spends the
> conservative tail to buy mean accuracy, and the control shows it was not even
> learning anything that transfers.

So the headroom R-P1.35 measured is **not** closed by a generic regressor. Closing
it would need a loss that encodes containment — a one-sided/quantile objective on
the pessimistic side — which is a *different* card, not a retune of this one. By
this card's own rule ("a learned arm that does not beat both the deterministic
baseline and its shuffled control is reported as a negative and no further training
is done"), the learned arm is **stopped here**.
