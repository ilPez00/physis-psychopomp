# PREREG — R-P1.33 rotated occluders: membership survives, the estimator does not (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.33.

`docs/LAW.md` names this as the remaining geometric gap: *rotated or curved
occluders (they need polygon membership)*. This card closes the **rotated** half
and tests two things that could go either way:

1. does the law's **membership** form survive geometry that is not axis-aligned —
   and does a **bounding-box** approximation visibly fail on it?
2. does the **rect estimator** of R-P1.29–R-P1.32 still behave? Its one-signed
   ("always optimistic") property was measured on axis-aligned rectangles; a
   diamond has corners inside its own bounding box, so a rect estimate should
   **over**-cover it. If so, the one-signed law is geometry-specific, not
   universal — and this card is where that shows.

## Instrument

- `SimConfig.poly_o: Tuple[Tuple[Tuple[x, y], ...], ...]` — static polygon
  blockers for observer 1, membership by ray casting (convex or not).
- `law.is_blind(config, point, t)` — the general membership form:
  `blind = blocked_by_observer_1 AND blocked_by_observer_2`, which needs no
  clipping and reduces to `(o ∪ extra) ∩ o2` for rectangles.
- `transform_scene` extended to polygons, so the frame-covariance result can be
  re-checked on non-rectangular geometry.

## World

`o = (40,60,35,65)` (rectangle), `o2 = (30,92,20,90)` (wide, so observer 2 sees
everything and the blind set is observer 1's shape), plus a **diamond** with
centre `(75,50)` and half-diagonal 8: vertices `(75,42), (83,50), (75,58), (67,50)`.

Note the diamond's **bounding box** `(67,83,42,58)` contains four corner points
that are **outside** the polygon: `(69,44)`, `(81,44)`, `(69,56)`, `(81,56)`.

Task points (`t* = 90`, one body each, staggered starts and per-body speeds so
each is at its point):

| point | inside the diamond? | inside its bbox? |
|---|---|---|
| `(75,50)` | yes | yes |
| `(69,44)` `(81,44)` `(69,56)` `(81,56)` | **no** | yes |
| `(64,50)` | no | no |

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (membership survives rotation).** For all six points, `is_blind`-based
  prediction equals the attribution result. *One mismatch falsifies.*
- **P2 (the bounding box does not).** A rule that treats the diamond as its
  bounding box mispredicts **at least the four corner points**: it calls them
  blind where they are identifiable. A bounding-box story cannot survive a
  rotation, which is the point of the card.
- **P3 (scene covariance extends to polygons).** Under the exact 90° scene
  symmetry — polygon vertices and bodies transformed together — the six outcomes
  are unchanged. *One mismatch falsifies.*
- **P4 (the rect estimator becomes pessimistic — predicted, not assumed).** Run
  the R-P1.29 estimator on a corpus that crosses the diamond: it returns a
  rectangle, and that rectangle **over-covers** the polygon, so at least one of
  the four corner points is called blind by the estimate while the truth says
  identifiable. If this holds it is the **first pessimistic self-model error**
  measured here, i.e. the one-signed property of R-P1.29–R-P1.32 is specific to
  axis-aligned geometry.
  *Falsified if* the estimate makes no pessimistic error on the corners.

## Prior

**0.85** for P1 and **0.9** for P2 (membership is a predicate; the bbox failure is
geometry). **0.7** for P3 (the symmetry maps the polygon exactly, but bodies must
land on their points after transformation, which is where an off-by-one could
hide). **0.65** for P4: the estimate's `x` bounds come from the widest crossings,
which is exactly the diamond's waist, so over-coverage is expected — but the row
function for `y` may shrink it enough to miss the corners.

## Rules

- No retune. A falsified prediction is a receipt.
- Curved occluders remain out of scope: a polygon approximates a curve to the
  step tolerance, and no curved shape is tested here.

## Execution status — run 1, 2026-11-22: P1/P2/P3 UPHELD, P4 FALSIFIED

| point | law | bbox rule | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| (75, 50) centre | tie | tie | **tie** ✓ |
| (69, 44) corner | unique | **tie** | **unique** ✓ |
| (81, 44) corner | unique | **tie** | **unique** ✓ |
| (69, 56) corner | unique | **tie** | **unique** ✓ |
| (81, 56) corner | unique | **tie** | **unique** ✓ |
| (64, 50) outside | unique | unique | **unique** ✓ |

- **P1 UPHELD** — the membership law predicts all six points exactly on a rotated
  occluder, and it does so with the *same* predicate as for rectangles
  (`is_blind` = every observer blocked; no clipping, no special case).
- **P2 UPHELD** — a bounding-box rule fails on **all four corners**, exactly as
  predicted: treating a rotated occluder as its envelope is a modelling error the
  instrument can see.
- **P3 UPHELD** — the 180° scene symmetry maps the polygon vertices and the bodies
  together and leaves all six outcomes unchanged, so the frame-covariance result
  extends to non-rectangular geometry.
- **P4 FALSIFIED — and the diagnosis is better than the prediction.** The rect
  estimator does not over-cover the diamond; it returns **`None`**. In its corpus,
  **15 of 23** crossings were labelled **`sweep`** because a polygon-caused
  visibility change has no rectangle edge to name, and the estimator reads only
  `left`/`right`/`bottom`/`top` — so three quarters of the evidence about the
  diamond is unusable to it, and the `y` edges of the rectangle below never got
  crossed at all.

### The finding

> The **law** generalises to rotated occluders as plain set membership, with no
> new apparatus. The **estimator** does not: it is rectangle-shaped by
> construction, and a rotated occluder is *invisible* to it while being perfectly
> visible to the law.

That is the first hard limit in the self-model story, and it is **representational,
not directional**: the one-signed optimism of R-P1.29–R-P1.32 was not tested here,
so it is neither confirmed nor refuted for non-rect geometry. My prediction of a
pessimistic error assumed the estimator would fit a rectangle around the diamond;
it cannot, because the crossings that would constrain it carry no side label.

### What this names for the next card

A shape-aware estimator needs **crossing normals for polygon edges** — the sim
already knows which polygon edge a body crossed, it simply does not label it. That
is a labelling change plus a polygon fit, not new physics.
