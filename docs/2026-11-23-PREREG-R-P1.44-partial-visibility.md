# PREREG — R-P1.44 partial visibility: where the law stops being exact (2026-11-23)

Status: **registered before the run**. Claim `f142a2b6-c9d5-44ec-a248-afb780814563`
registered in the store before the run (required from 2026-11-23 by the checklist).

Nine cards have tested the law and it has held. The most informative thing left is
the opposite: **a condition under which it must fail**, stated precisely enough to
measure. The instrument has always treated a body as a *point*: hidden iff its centre
is inside the blind region. A real body has radius `r`, and if any part of it is
outside, the observer still sees something — a sliver of edge, a moving boundary. So
the blind region for a body of radius `r` is the blind region for a point **shrunk by
`r`** on every edge, and a centre-based predicate over-predicts blindness in a band of
width `r`.

## Instrument

`SimConfig.visual_radius: float = 0.0` — the body is **fully hidden** only when its
whole disc is inside a blocker, i.e. the centre is inside the rect *and* at least `r`
from every rect edge. `0.0` reproduces every prior number exactly (the point model).

`law.is_blind` is **left alone** — it stays the centre-based predicate, because the
card is about that predicate's scope, not about a new law. The instrument and the law
therefore disagree by construction in the band, which is the measurement.

Polygons keep the point model (`visual_radius` applies to rectangles only); that is a
limit of this card, stated rather than hidden.

## Cells

`o = (40,60,35,65)`, `o2 = (46,54,35,65)` → point-level blind region `(46,54,35,65)`.
Body radius `r ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 1.0}` (the simulator's own collision radius is 1.0).

Task points, a body on each, `t* = 90`, one per episode:

| point | distance to the nearest blind edge | prediction at `r = 0.5` |
|---|---|---|
| `(50, 45)` | 4.0 (deep inside) | law "blind", instrument blind |
| `(53.7, 45)` | **0.3** (inside the band) | law "blind", instrument **sees it** |
| `(54.3, 45)` | **0.3** outside | law "identifiable", instrument sees it |
| `(60, 45)` | 6.0 outside | law "identifiable", instrument sees it |

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **V1 (the band exists).** At `r = 0.5`, the law mispredicts for the **inside-band**
  event `(53.7, 45)`: it says blind, and the observer records the body (its exit event
  exists), so attribution identifies it. **At least that one** misprediction, on that
  specific point.
- **V2 (outside the band the law is exact).** At `r = 0.5`, `(50, 45)` is blind by
  both, and `(54.3, 45)` / `(60, 45)` are identifiable by both. No misprediction
  outside the band.
- **V3 (the band scales with `r`).** The number of mispredicted task points is
  **monotone non-decreasing** in `r` across `{0.0, 0.5, 1.0}`, and is **0 at `r = 0`**.
- **V4 (the repair is exactly "whole body hidden").** Re-running the law's predicate
  with the blind region **shrunk by `r`** predicts every task point correctly at every
  `r` — so the failure is the *definition* of hidden, not the coverage story.

### The finding

> The law is exact for a **point** body. For a body of radius `r` the blind region is
> the point-level one **shrunk by `r`** on every edge, and a centre-based predicate
> over-predicts blindness in a band of width `r` along every blind edge. Shrinking by
> `r` repairs it exactly.

That is a *scope condition* the whole program had been assuming without stating: nine
cards measured "hidden" as "the centre is inside". They are correct for the point
model they declared; the condition is now measured rather than implicit.

### The bug that nearly hid the result

The first patch shrank **only observer 1's** blockers. `observable` ends with
`second is not None and not inside_rect(second, …)`, so observer 2's rect kept hiding
the point on the unshrunk rule and **nothing** became visible — the card measured
"no band" at every radius and would have reported V1/V3/V4 falsified. The fix applies
the same `fully_inside` test to both observers. This is the second consecutive card
where a partial patch to a shared predicate produced a plausible wrong answer
(R-P1.43's lag helper was the first), and both were caught by the card's own control
cells rather than by review.
