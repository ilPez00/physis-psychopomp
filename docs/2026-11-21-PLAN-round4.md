# Plan — empty worlds, noise-aware scoring, abstention (2026-11-21, round 4)

Status: **PLAN**, written before execution. Predecessors: rounds 2 and 3.

## What the check found

`summarize` reports `contact_required: False` for a log with **zero** consistent
worlds (`n_worlds: 0`). "No consistent world exists" and "the balls did not
contact" are different statements, and the report collapses them. A teleport
log, an inconsistent log, or a sensor coarser than the filter's tolerance all
land in the same bucket and are read downstream as a negative label — the third
instance of the fabrication class (oracle truth → undecided M3 label → empty
enumeration).

## R1 — empty enumeration is undecidable, not negative

`contact_required` is decided only when the episode is **cleared and at least
one world exists**. Zero worlds must read as `None` with `n_worlds: 0`
alongside, so a caller cannot mistake "inconsistent log" for "no contact".

Gate: a teleport-shaped log reports `None`; a valid log still reports `False`;
`test_teleport_zero_worlds` keeps its world count. Mutation: restoring the old
expression makes the new test RED.

## R2 — noise-aware scoring

`m3_accuracy` and `m2_baseline` take the same `tol` the filter takes. Without
it, scoring a noisy world at the default `1e-4` measures the collapse (R1's
bucket) rather than the sensor. Default stays `1e-4`, so every existing number
is unchanged.

Gate: both accept `tol`; the shipped corpora produce identical numbers at the
default.

## R3 — abstention is a reported number, not a hope

The A2 probe already refuses ties internally (`len(winners) == 1`), but the
report does not say how often it decided. Add `decided` / `ambiguous` to the
report and print them, so a degrading sensor shows up as **falling decision
rate** instead of a silently lower accuracy.

Gate: `scripts/a3-degradation.py` prints decided/ambiguous per level; on the
clean corpus decided = 6, and decided falls as noise rises. Not a registered
claim.

## Order and stop rule

R1 → R2 → R3. Two FAILs on one item: record and move on. Each lands as its own
commit with the gate output quoted.

  **and** at least one world exists; zero worlds now reports `None`. Mutation

  `test_zero_worlds_is_undecidable_not_no_contact` RED.
- **R2 DONE.** `m2_baseline` and `m3_accuracy` accept `tol`. Measured on a
  noise=2 episode: the strict default excludes it (`excluded 1`, nothing
  explains the log at 1e-4) while `tol=2.0` scores it (`excluded 0`). Shipped

- **R3 DONE, and its expectation was WRONG in an informative way.** The plan
  expected the decision rate to fall with noise. Measured instead: the probe is
  **decided 6/6 at every noise level**, accuracy falling 1.00 → 0.83 → 0.33 —
  i.e. its errors are *confident* errors, not ties.
  Added `abstain_margin`, then measured whether a margin buys accuracy:

  | margin | decided | accuracy among decided |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | 6/6 | 0.33 |
  | 1 | 5/6 | 0.40 |
  | 2 | 3/6 | 0.33 |
  | 5 | 1/6 | 0.00 |
  | 10 | 0/6 | — |

  Margin abstention trades coverage without buying accuracy: the score's
  confidence carries no information about whether it is right. (Caveat: the
  margin≥5 rows rest on one decided episode each; they are not evidence of a
  trend, only of the small sample.) This is the strongest argument yet for a
  learned arm — not that physics is weak, but that its *confidence* is
  uninformative at coarse resolution.
- `decided` / `ambiguous` are now part of the probe report and printed by
  `scripts/a3-degradation.py`.
