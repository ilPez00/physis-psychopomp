# PREREG — R-P1.45 partial visibility for polygon occluders (2026-11-23)

Status: **registered before the run**. Claim `5d07b83d-df00-490e-9e3b-e34856d2913b`
registered in the store before the run.

R-P1.44 measured that the centre-based law over-predicts blindness in a band of width
`r` along rectangle edges, and repaired it by shrinking the blind region. It left one
case explicitly out of scope: **polygons kept the point model**. This card removes that
limit for convex polygons and states the one thing it still cannot claim.

## Instrument

`SimConfig.visual_radius` now applies to `poly_o` as well: a body is fully hidden by a
polygon only when the point is inside **and** at least `r` from the polygon's boundary
(distance to the nearest edge segment). `0.0` keeps every prior number exact.

Why this is not the same computation as the rectangle case: a rectangle's edges are
axis-aligned and independent, so `x0 + r ≤ x ≤ x1 − r` is exact for an interior point.
A slanted edge has no such decomposition, and the nearest boundary near a **corner** is
the vertex, not an edge — so the test is distance-to-segment, not axis comparison.

## World and cells

`o = (40,60,35,65)`; `o2 = (30,92,20,90)` (wide, so the blind set is the polygon);
a **diamond** `(75,42),(83,50),(75,58),(67,50)` — slanted edges, and vertices that a
distance-to-edge shortcut would get wrong.

Points on lane `y = 50` (the diamond's widest section, so the horizontal distance to the
slanted boundary is the perpendicular distance divided by `cos45`):

|---|---|---|---|
| `(75, 50)` centre | 5.657 | yes | fully hidden both ways |
| `(82.0, 50)` | **0.707** | yes | hidden both ways |
| `(82.6, 50)` | **0.283** | yes | **law hidden, instrument sees it** |
| `(83.0, 50)` vertex | 0.0 | **no** (ray-cast excludes the boundary) | visible both ways |

**AMENDMENT 1 — before any verdict.** The first draft of this table gave the
distances as 1.41 / 0.99 for the two inner points and expected the radio **vertex**
`(83, 50)` to be called blind by the law. Both were wrong, and the instrument said so
directly while this card was being built: the perpendicular distance near a vertex is
`Δx·cos45`, not `Δx/cos45` (a factor of two, not of √2 — 0.283 where I wrote 0.99), and
the ray-cast test excludes a point exactly on the boundary, so the vertex is visible
under **both** predicates. The band cell is therefore `(82.6, 50)`, and the vertex becomes
a **boundary case**: excluded from the verdicts under the standing 1e-6 rule, kept in the
table as the visible control it turned out to be.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (the band exists on a slanted edge).** At `r = 0.5` the point `(82.6, 50)` — 0.283
  from the boundary — is seen by the instrument while the point-based law calls it blind.
- **P2 (the band, not more).** At `r = 0.5`, `(75,50)` and `(82.0,50)` are blind under
  both predicates, and `(64,50)` is identifiable under both: no misprediction away from
  the band. The vertex `(83,50)` is a boundary case, excluded and reported.
- **P3 (`r = 0` is the point model).** Zero mispredictions at `r = 0` for all five
  points.
- **P4 (the repair is an inward erosion).** Eroding the polygon by `r` (shifting each
  edge inward along its normal and keeping the intersection) predicts **every** point
  correctly at every radius — for this **convex** diamond.

## Prior

**0.8** for P1/P2/P3 (the geometry is direct and the instrument is observable).
**0.65** for P4: erosion-by-`r` is exact for a convex polygon, but the points sit on the
axis of symmetry where the two slanted edges meet, which is precisely where a
half-plane-intersection implementation can be off by a rounding choice.

## Rules

- No retune. A falsified prediction is a receipt.
- One polygon, five points, three radii — declared here.
- **Non-convex erosion is not claimed**: an inward offset of a non-convex polygon can
  split or delete parts of it, and that is a different (and harder) computation.

### The finding

> The partial-visibility rule is **shape-independent**: a body is identifiable iff its
> **whole disc** is outside the blind set, and the repair is the same in both cases —
> shrink a rectangle, **erode** a convex polygon, by `r`. Nothing about the slanted edge
> needed a new idea; it needed the distance-to-**segment** test rather than an
> axis comparison, because near a vertex the nearest boundary point is the vertex.

### What the amendment caught

The prereg's first table had the band at `0.99` (a factor of two too large) and expected
the **vertex** to be a law-misprediction. The instrument disagreed while the card was
being built: the perpendicular distance near a vertex is `Δx·cos45 = 0.283`, and a
ray-cast excludes a point exactly on the boundary, so the vertex is visible under **both**
predicates. Amendment 1 fixed both before any verdict, and the vertex stayed in the table
as the visible control it turned out to be. Had it stayed as written, P1 and P2 would have
been reported as falsified on arithmetic.

### Limits

Convex polygons only. An inward offset of a **non-convex** polygon can split or delete
parts of it, which is a different computation and is not claimed. `r` applies to
`poly_o` and to the observers' rectangles; a body larger than a blind cell is out of
scope by construction.
