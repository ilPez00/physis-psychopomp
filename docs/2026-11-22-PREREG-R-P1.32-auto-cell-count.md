# PREREG — R-P1.32 estimating the cell count itself (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.32.

R-P1.31 measured what happens when the observer is told the **wrong** number of
blind cells. It was *told* the number, which is the one thing an observer does not
get for free. This card removes that: the count comes from the same crossing
evidence as the geometry, and the question becomes whether the supplied structural
prior was load-bearing at all.

## Instrument

`estimate_blind_cells_auto(episodes, step)` — clusters the crossing bounds along
`x` and reports however many cells the evidence supports, with no `n_cells`
argument. Clusters separated by more than `2·step` are distinct cells; fewer
clusters than cells means the corpus never crossed them, and that is reported
(`coverage`), not papered over.

## Worlds and corpora

- **islands world** (R-P1.31): `o = (40,60,35,65)`, extra `(70,78,35,65)`,
  `o2 = (44,84,20,90)` → blind `(44,60) ∪ (70,78)`, gap at `60–70`;
  tasks `x ∈ {42, 44, 65, 74}` on lane `y = 45`, drifts as before.
- **full corpus** — horizontal and vertical sweeps crossing **both** cells;
- **sparse corpus** — the same sweeps restricted to `x ∈ [36, 58]`, so the island
  is **never crossed**;
- **single-cell world** (R-P1.29): `o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, corpus as R-P1.29 — a
  control that the auto count does not invent cells.

## Predictions (fixed now — comparisons and counts, not transcribed numbers)

- **P1 (the count is recoverable).** On the full islands corpus the auto estimator
  returns exactly **2** cells, each contained in its truth and within one step —
  i.e. the same answer as being told `n_cells = 2`.
- **P2 (no invented cells).** On the single-cell world the auto estimator returns
  exactly **1** cell within one step. Noise-free evidence must not manufacture
  structure.
- **P3 (the structural prior was not load-bearing).** An agent that estimates both
  the count and the geometry plans at the **oracle score** on the islands task set
  — no worse than R-P1.31's agent that was told the count.
- **P4 (thin evidence fails optimistically, and silently).** On the sparse corpus
  the auto estimator returns **1** cell (it never saw the island), and an agent
  planning from it scores **strictly lower** than the full-corpus agent. Nothing
  in the evidence marks the model as incomplete — the failure is the same
  optimistic direction as every other self-model error measured here, now with a
  practical consequence: **you have to sweep your whole field of view to know your
  blind spots.**

## Prior

**0.8** for P1/P2/P3 (clustering is exact on noise-free bounds, and the two edges
are 10 units apart against a 0.6 threshold). **0.65** for P4's strict-lower
comparison: it needs the missing island to cost a task event, and the drift that
hides `74` might coincide with the drift the one-cell model happens to prefer —
which is exactly how R-P1.31's P2 fell.

### The finding

> The blind cells and their **count** are both recoverable from the observer's own
> crossings, so self-knowledge needs no structural prior. What it does need is
> **coverage**: a corpus that never crossed a cell produces a confidently
> incomplete model, and the only defence is sweeping the whole field of view.

That is the last piece of the arc: coverage of the *world* decides identifiability
(R-P1.12–R-P1.20), coverage of *yourself* decides whether you know where you are
blind (R-P1.29–R-P1.32), and both fail optimistically when under-sampled.

### Limits

Two cells, disjoint, ordered along `x`, noise-free; a cell that is never crossed
is invisible to the estimator by construction (that is P4, not a bug). The count is
recovered from `x`-clustering only — a blind set whose cells differ only in `y`
would need the `y` bounds to separate them, which the horizontal sweeps here do not
provide.
