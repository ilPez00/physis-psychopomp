# Plan — the statistic survey: is any observable signal there at all? (2026-11-21, round 6)

Status: **PLAN**, written before execution. Predecessor: round 5.

## Why

Round 5 gave the ratio rule (`sep/noise ≥ 4` → 0.83, `≤ 2` → 0.33 plateau). The
open question that decides A3 is narrower than "train a model":

> in the plateau cell, does **any** per-world statistic rank the true world
> better than the y-continuity residual does — and do statistics that fail
> disagree with each other?

If every cheap statistic sits at or below the residual, a learned arm has no
observable handle and the task is unjustifiable. If several statistics are each
weak but *disagree*, a combination has something to learn from — that, and only
that, justifies an arm.

## W1 — keep it deterministic and unfitted

Survey rankings under statistics a reader can re-derive by hand:

1. `y_residual` — the A2 residual (existing);
2. `x_residual` — |predicted x − observed x| under constant velocity;
3. `y_order` — |Δy| alone, ignoring velocity;
4. `duration` — |observed crossing time − entry-to-exit time implied by speed|;
5. `vote` — rank-1 by majority of the four.

**No fitting.** A hand-rolled classifier would be a new claim with no review;
an unfitted survey is a measurement.

## W2 — measure the plateau and the healthy cell

`sep/noise = 2/2` (ratio 1, plateau) and `6/2` (ratio 3, healthy), 6 episodes
each, chance 0.25. Report per-statistic top-1 and whether the truth is ever
rank-1 under any statistic.

## W3 — the actionable output

One sentence with a number: "at ratio ≤ 2, the best unfitted statistic reaches
X", which is what an A3 registration has to beat. Recorded in the round-6 plan
and the FIXES document; not registered as a claim.

## Gate

`scripts/statistic-survey.py` prints the table; a test pins the healthy cell
(the survey must find the truth there) and the plateau cell (no statistic
exceeds the stated bound), so a future change to the instrument shows up as a
number that moved.

## Execution status — 2026-11-21 (appended after measurement)

- **W1/W2 DONE.** `psychopomp.probes.statistic_survey` scores four unfitted
  statistics plus a majority vote; `scripts/statistic-survey.py` prints the
  table. Output is identical under `PYTHONHASHSEED=0/1/2` (md5 of stdout), so the
  survey itself is cross-process stable.

  | separation / noise | ratio | y_residual | x_residual | y_order | best covered |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 6 / 2 | 3.0 | 0.67 | 0.67 | **1.00** | **1.00** |
  | 2 / 2 | 1.0 | 0.33 | 0.67 | **1.00** | **1.00** |
  | 2 / 5 | 0.4 | 0.33 | 0.67 | 0.25 | **0.67** |

- **The round-5 reading is corrected.** "The plateau at ratio <= 2" was a
  property of **the y-continuity residual**, not of the observation: dropping the
  velocity term and ranking by `|dy|` alone scores **1.00** at ratio 1.0, where
  the residual scores 0.33. Adding `v*dt*steps` to the prediction amplifies
  measurement noise instead of using the straight-line constraint. So identity is
  *not* unrecoverable in that cell -- my estimator was.
  Consequence: an A3 task still has no headroom at ratio >= 1, but the reason is
  "a trivial statistic already solves it", not "nothing can".
- **The only candidate cell is ratio <= 0.4**: no covered statistic reaches 1.00,
  the best (`x_residual`, 0.67) beats chance 0.25, and `y_order` collapses to
  0.25. That is where a better estimator could win -- and it is the cell an A3
  registration would have to name, with its own floor re-measured.
- **Coverage is part of the result.** `speed_delta` reports 1.00 on one decision
  in the same cells; a 1.00 with `n=1` is coverage, not accuracy. The test asserts
  only statistics that decided every episode, which is why the table's last column
  is "best covered" and not "best".
