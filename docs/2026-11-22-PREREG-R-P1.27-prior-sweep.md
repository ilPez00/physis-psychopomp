# PREREG — R-P1.27 the cost of a confident prior, all five peaks (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.27.
R-P1.26's P3 picked **one** misplaced prior and was falsified by it. The fix is
not a better guess, it is a declared sweep: every peaked prior in the belief set,
chosen here, measured once, reported as a distribution rather than as a story.

## Design

R-P1.21/R-P1.24/R-P1.26 harness, unchanged: `o = (40,60,35,65)`,
`o2 = (46,54,35,65)`, tasks `x ∈ {42,46,48,52}` on lane `y = 45`, `t* = 90`,
drifts `v ∈ {−1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1}`, belief set `Δ = {−20,−10,0,+10,+20}`.

Five Bayesian planners, each with mass **0.8** on one `δ ∈ Δ` and the rest
uniform. Plus minimax (`−1.0`, measured 3/4 in R-P1.24) and the oracle (4/4).

**All five are declared now**, so no peak is selected after seeing a result. This
is the whole point of the card: R-P1.26's single peak proved nothing either way.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (the sweep has real spread).** The five peaks' measured costs (regret
  against the oracle) are **not all equal**: at least one peak costs 2 events and
  at least one costs 0. A confident prior is not uniformly safe or uniformly
  dangerous.
- **P2 (the peak on the truth is free).** The peak at `δ = 0` has cost **0**
  (it picks `+1.0`, 4/4).
- **P3 (the hedge sits in the middle, not at the top).** The minimax plan's cost
  (1 event) is **≤ the mean** of the five peaks' costs, and **≥ the minimum**:
  hedging is neither dominated nor dominating — it is the plan you choose without
  a prior, and it is beaten by an honest prior and by lucky priors.
- **P4 (the law underlies every plan).** For each of the five chosen drifts,
  predicted identifiability under the true geometry equals the measured count.

## Prior

**0.65.** P1/P2/P4 follow from the table; P3 is a real claim about where the
hedge sits and could fail if all five peaks happen to cost ≤ 1 while the hedge
costs 1 (then hedging is at the top and the earlier R-P1.24 framing needs
softening).

## Rules

- No retune, and **no peak added or removed** after the result.
- One line per peak, reported as a table; the mean is reported with the spread,
  never alone.

Costs: `{−20: 1, −10: 1, 0: 0, +10: 0, +20: 1}`, **mean 0.60, spread 1**, hedge 1.

- **P1 FALSIFIED.** I predicted a spread of at least 2 with at least one peak
  costing 2 events. No peak costs more than 1.
- **P2 UPHELD** — the peak on the truth is free.
- **P3 FALSIFIED, and it is the informative one.** I predicted the hedge would sit
  between the best peak and the mean. It sits **above** the mean (1 > 0.60) and
  **equal** to the worst peak: in this world the hedge is **dominated in
  expectation** by the average confident prior.
- **P4 UPHELD.**

A second prediction of mine rested on the plan table alone and was wrong twice in
a row: I expected the peak at `δ = +10` to prefer `0.0`; the residual weights make
`+1.0` the maximum (3.9 vs 3.8). Reading one row of a table is not reading the
expectation.

### The honest synthesis across R-P1.24 / R-P1.26 / R-P1.27

- the hedge beats the **worst naive plan** (R-P1.24: 3/4 vs 1/4);
- with a **flat** prior, expected utility *is* the hedge (R-P1.26);
- with an **honest** peaked prior, expected utility beats it with zero regret
  (R-P1.26, and `δ = 0` here);
- across **all five** peaks the mean cost is **0.6**, and the worst peak costs the
  same as the hedge (this card).

So the correct statement is narrower than "hedging pays":

> Hedging buys protection against a **worst case this world does not produce**.
> Every confident prior tested lands within 1 event of the oracle, so minimax is
> not dominated by robustness requirements here — it is *pessimism with nothing
> adversarial to defend against*.

That is a real narrowing of R-P1.24's claim, and `docs/LAW.md` is corrected to say
so. A world where belief errors are genuinely adversarial (a δ that pushes the
planner onto a 1/4 drift) is **not** exhibited by this geometry.
