## The three seams

1. **Composite mask.** Two rectangles that overlap form a non-convex region whose
   *union* is the blind area but whose *seams* (the interior edges where they meet)
   are not boundaries of the region. An estimator that treats each rectangle as
   contributing its own four edges will invent interior edges that are not there.
2. **Reflex corner.** An L-shaped mask (two rectangles meeting at a corner) has a
   concave vertex. A body passing through the notch is visible for part of its
   path and hidden for part, but never crosses a rectangle's edge in the usual
   sense.
3. **Finite-radius labels.** With `visual_radius > 0` a body is hidden only when
   its whole disc is inside a mask, so an event can occur while the disc is
   *partly* visible. R-P1.44 measured that "a visible sliver suffices" for the
   identifiability verdict; what was never measured is whether the **edge estimate**
   (`estimate_blind_region`) shifts when the body has extent.

## Design

Everything else is R-P1.9's: one pair, `HIDDEN_KICK` at t=60, 5 seeds, the
estimator and the attribution the lane already uses.

- **Arm A — single mask, point model.** The control. Must reproduce the lane's
  known single-observer tie.
- **Arm B — union of two overlapping rectangles** (composite). Same total blind
  *area* as A is impossible, so the comparison is: does the estimator's inferred
  rectangle have interior edges, and does that change the verdict?
- **Arm C — L-shaped mask** as a `poly_o` polygon (or two rectangles meeting at a
  corner). One event placed in the notch, one outside it. This is the arm R-P1.45
  did not run: it measured whether a point is visible, not what the evidence does
  to the verdict.
- **Arm D — `visual_radius = 1.0`** on A's geometry, so the effective mask is
  eroded by one unit. The event that was hidden at radius 0 becomes partly visible.

The point of the card is not to build a polygon estimator. It is to answer, per
seam, **does the simplification change a verdict** — because a "no" is what
licenses keeping the rectangle model, and a "yes" localises what needs building.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the control is stable.** Arm A reproduces the single-observer tie
  (ambiguity 2) on 5/5 seeds, so the fixture is anchored before anything changes.
- **P2 — a composite mask changes the inferred geometry, not yet the verdict.**
  The estimator's rectangle under arm B differs from arm A by more than the
  fixture's tolerance on the **interior seam** (it reports an edge where the union
  has none), but the tie/unique verdict is unchanged, because a tie is a tie for
  any covering region. Predicted: geometry differs, verdict does not.
- **P3 — a reflex corner changes the verdict for a notch event.** An event inside
  the notch is visible through the opening, so single-observer attribution should
  become **unique** where it tied under a solid mask of the same area. A reflex
  region is not a covering region, and the verdict should follow coverage, not
  area — the R-P1.10 rule, restated for a non-convex mask.
- **P4 — finite radius moves the edge estimate by exactly the radius.** With
  `visual_radius = r`, the estimated blind rectangle is eroded by `r` on each
  side, so the signed margin shifts by `−r` per edge relative to radius 0.
  Measured as four edge margins, not as a verdict.
- **P5 — no arm leaks the oracle.** The oracle log is identical across A–D.

Any prediction may fail and is kept. **P3 is the load-bearing one**: if a reflex
notch does *not* change the verdict, then area really is a sufficient statistic
and the rectangle model is safer than it looks.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say which simplification is safe on this fixture, per seam, with the
  measured margins.
- It **may not** claim a polygon estimator, a planner change, or general
  non-convex support. This is a seam study: one geometry per seam, 5 seeds.
- It **may not** retune R-P1.10 or R-P1.44. Their receipts stand; this card
  restates their rule for shapes they did not test.
- `E6` stays closed.

## Controls and honesty

- Arm A is the anchor and must reproduce the known single-observer result.
- Each arm varies **one** thing from A. The reflex arm keeps the rectangle count
  and the covered area as close as the geometry allows, and the receipt prints
  both areas so "same area, different verdict" is checkable.
- The plate/control pair the todo asks for is exactly P2 (geometry moves) plus P3
  (verdict moves): a seam that changes geometry but not a decision is reported as
  a control, not as a defect.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

Two-rectangle composites only; no polygon with a hole; no oblique edges. One
predicate, one pair, five seeds. Extending the simulator to general polygons is
out of scope and is what a "yes" on P2/P3 would justify building.

### The result P2/P3 gave, stated plainly

`estimate_blind_region` **returns the bounding rectangle of the observed
crossings**, so a union and a notch that produce the same outer crossings produce
the same four numbers. The estimator cannot represent a non-convex mask and does
not try. That is the seam this card found: it is not in `sim.py` (which models
polygons and unions correctly, per R-P1.45 and R-P1.20/23) but in
`selfmodel.py`, whose `BlindEstimate` **is** a rectangle.

So the answer to "do non-rectangular masks change the attribution verdict" is
**not on this evidence**: the verdict is computed from the raw observations by
`attribute`, and in both arms the outer crossings dominate, so the tie survives.
Whether a notch *can* change it needs an event whose visibility differs — and the
event here `(46, 45)` is inside the solid part of the L, not in the notch.

### Three fixture defects found before any of that was readable

1. **A single pair gives no vertical samples.** `_rect_bounds` needs `bottom` and
   `top` crossings; a horizontal-only corpus leaves them at 0 and `as_rect()`
   returns `None`. Two attempts produced "no estimate" and every geometry
   prediction read FAILED against a missing value.
2. **Vertical columns must start outside and run long enough to exit.** With 200
   steps the columns entered and never left, so `top` stayed 0. `steps = 400`.
3. **`visual_radius = 1.0` erodes the mask past the corpus start points**, so arm
   D has *zero* samples of any kind. That is a fixture bug, not a radius result,
   and P4 is reported FAILED with the reason rather than as evidence.

P5 failed because arm C's `poly_o` changes the *observable* log (a body clipping
the notch reports differently) while the oracle should not move — the check
compared full oracle sequences including positions and the polygon's presence
altered a `wall_bounce` time. That is a real ambiguity in the control: "no arm
touches the oracle" needs comparing the *intended* events, not every derived one.

### What this card establishes, and what it does not

- **Establishes:** `session`'s `BlindEstimate` is a rectangle by construction, so
  seam studies of composite or reflex masks cannot be run through it. Any future
  card about non-convex masks needs either a polygon estimator or a decision
  computed directly from observations — and `attribute` already does the latter.
- **Does not establish:** that a reflex mask cannot change a verdict. The notch
  event was never placed in the notch (the fixture's event is at `(46, 45)`,
  inside the solid arm of the L). That is the next card's job and it is a
  one-line fixture change, not a new mechanism.

`E6` stays closed. This card spent its budget on fixture defects and reports them
rather than the prediction it set out to test.

## Correction (2026-11-27, after the run): arm C was never a notch

The run above says arm C is an "L polygon with a notch" and reports the mask area
as 420. **Both statements are wrong about what the simulator did.**

`SimConfig.o` defaults to a rectangle and `observable` treats a point as hidden
when **any** of `o`, `extra_o` or `poly_o` covers it. The occluders are a
**union**, so `poly_o` can only ever *add* mask. Arm C therefore ran as
"full rectangle ∪ L polygon", whose covered area is the rectangle's 600 — not
420 — and which has **no notch at all**. The shoelace area in the receipt
measures the polygon, not the mask.

Confirmed directly:

```
cfg = SimConfig(poly_o=(L_POLY,))          # default o = (40,60,35,65)
observable(cfg, 46, 52)  ->  False          # inside the would-be notch: HIDDEN
```

so P3 ("the reflex notch changes the verdict") was never tested: there was no
notch, and the event `(46, 45)` sat in the solid arm in every arm.

A mask **with** a hole needs the mandatory first rectangle parked away from the
action:

```
cfg = SimConfig(o=(2, 4, 2, 4), poly_o=(L_POLY,))
observable(cfg, 46, 45) -> False    # solid arm
observable(cfg, 46, 55) -> True     # notch: VISIBLE
observable(cfg, 55, 55) -> False    # solid arm
```

That is the fixture R-P1.71 uses. The estimator finding in this card stands
unchanged — `BlindEstimate` is still a rectangle by construction — but the
verdict half of the card measured a solid rectangle three times.

## Prior art this card failed to cite (correction, 2026-11-27)

**R-P1.33 (`rotated-occluders-20261122`) had already measured this**, five days
before the card was written. Its receipt's finding:

> "the rect self-model estimator does not generalise at all — it returns `None`
> because polygon-caused crossings carry no edge label. The limit is
> representational, not directional."

And the roadmap row for it says the same in one line: *"The estimator returns
`None` — 15 of 23 crossings were polygon-caused `sweep` labels it cannot read.
The law generalises; the estimator is rectangle-shaped."*

So this card's headline — that the seam is in `selfmodel.py` rather than
`sim.py`, and that `BlindEstimate` is rectangle-shaped — is a **confirmation of a
recorded result, not a discovery**. The card was written without reading that row,
which is the same failure the roadmap-receipt gate exists to catch, one level up:
not a claim without a receipt, a claim without a search.

What is genuinely additive here, stated narrowly:

- the **specific mechanism** in two places rather than one — R-P1.33 saw
  `sweep` labels on polygon crossings; this card also shows a **union of
  rectangles** (`extra_o`) producing an estimate identical to its bounding
  rectangle, which is the composite case R-P1.33 did not run;
- the **three fixture defects** in the estimator's sample accounting (a
  horizontal-only corpus, short runs that never exit, and a radius that erodes
  past the corpus start), which cost two runs here and are not in that receipt.

Nothing else. R-P1.71's notch result stands on its own footing — R-P1.33 did not
place an event in a hole — but it is a narrow addition beside a known limit, not
evidence that the limit was unknown.
