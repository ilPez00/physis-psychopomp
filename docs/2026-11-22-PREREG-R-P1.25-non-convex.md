# PREREG — R-P1.25 non-convex blind regions (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.25.

`docs/LAW.md` states its scope gap plainly: *non-rectangular or rotated
occluders are out of scope*. That is where a "coverage" story is most likely to
be a bounding-box story in disguise. This card removes half the gap — the
non-convex half — with a generalisation that does not need a polygon rasteriser,
and tests whether the law is really **set membership**.

## Instrument change

`SimConfig.extra_o: Tuple[rect, ...] = ()` — extra blockers for the **first**
observer. Visibility generalises from

    visible = p ∉ o      or  p ∉ o2

to

    visible = (p ∉ every rect of observer 1)  or  p ∉ o2

so the blind set is

    blind = (o ∪ extra_o) ∩ o2(t)

which is a **union** of rects intersected with a rect: with `extra_o` disjoint
from `o`, the blind region becomes two islands, and with `extra_o` touching `o`
it becomes an L. No polygons, no rasterisation, and the second observer keeps its
drift.

Rotated occluders remain **out of scope** (they would need polygon membership);
this card does not claim them.

## Grid

`o = (40, 60, 35, 65)`, `o2 = (30, 92, 20, 90)` (wide enough that the blind set
is exactly the shape of observer 1's blockers — see amendment 1; static).

| cell | `extra_o` | blind set | shape on the lanes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | `()` | `(40,60,35,65)` | one interval |
| `islands` | `((70,85,35,65),)` | `(40,60,35,65) ∪ (70,85,35,65)` | two intervals, **gap at 60–70** |
| `stacked` | `((40,60,70,85),)` | `(40,60,35,65) ∪ (40,60,70,85)` | same x-interval, **stacked in y** |

Event points, `t* = 90`, one body per point: **lane `y = 45`** at
`x ∈ {45, 55, 65, 75, 85}` and **lane `y = 75`** at `x = 50` (six bodies).

| point | `plain` | `islands` | `stacked` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (45, 45) | blind | blind | blind |
| (55, 45) | blind | blind | blind |

Predictions are computed by the law itself (`blind_cells` membership), not
hand-written per cell, but the *shape* claims below are hand-written and are what
the card is about.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (set membership, not bounding box).** In `islands` and `elbow`, the point
  `x = 65` — inside the **bounding box** of the blind set — is **identifiable**,
  while `x = 45, 55` are not. A bounding-box rule would call all three blind.
- **P2 (islands are independent).** In `islands`, `x = 75` and `x = 85` are
  unidentifiable (second island) while `x = 65` (between the islands) is
  identifiable: two disjoint blind components, each obeyed.
- **P3 (no regression).** In `plain`, `x = 45, 55` are unidentifiable and
  `x = 65, 75, 85` are identifiable.
- **P4 (the law and the instrument agree in every cell).** Measured
  identifiability equals `law.predict_identifiable` for all **15** cells
  (3 shapes × 5 points); boundary cells excluded under the standing 1e-6 rule and
  reported.
- **P5 (the generalisation is real).** The `islands` and `elbow` blind sets are
  **not** equal to their own bounding boxes — i.e. the world genuinely cannot be
  described by one rect.

## Prior

**0.8.** The law is a membership predicate by construction, so P1–P4 should hold;
the risk is instrumentation — the side labels and transition lookup assume
rects, and multi-rect blocking is exactly where an off-by-one rect could hide.

## AMENDMENT 1 — 2026-11-22, before the run

Two construction defects, no hypothesis or threshold touched:

1. **`o2` clipped the island.** With `o2 = (30, 70, 20, 75)` the second
   observer's rect ends at `x = 70`, so `(70, 85)` intersected with it degenerates
   to a line and the "island" vanishes. `o2` becomes `(30, 92, 20, 90)` — wide
   enough that the blind set *is* observer 1's shape, which is the whole point.
2. **Bodies started inside blockers.** With one lane and `x0 = task − 27`, three
   of five bodies began inside `o`. Bodies now start in the free left band at
   `x0 = 5 + 5i` with per-body speed `(task_i − x0_i) / (0.1·t*)`, so each is at
   its task point exactly at `t*` and every start is observable. Speeds increase
   with position, so the bodies never close on each other and cannot collide.

## AMENDMENT 2 — 2026-11-22, before any verdict

The first grid's shapes were placed where the measurement lane could never see
them: the "island" sat at `y ∈ [70, 85]` while events were on `y = 45`, so the
island was **vertically off-lane** and the card silently tested one interval
three times. That is a capacity failure of the prereg, caught by hand-written
expectations disagreeing with the law rather than by the law disagreeing with the
instrument.

Corrected: `islands` becomes `(70, 85, 35, 65)` — disjoint from `o`, same lane
band, producing a real gap at `60–70`; the former "elbow" becomes `stacked`
`(40, 60, 70, 85)` and is probed on a **second lane** `y = 75`, which is the only
way a vertically displaced rect can be tested. Six bodies (five on lane 45, one
on lane 75), three shapes, **18 cells**. Hypotheses and thresholds unchanged.

### Two check fixes, recorded

Both were *check statements* that contradicted the design, not thresholds:

1. P4 counted `scored == 18`, but the standing boundary rule **requires** the
   edge cell to be excluded, so the correct count is `scored + boundary == 18`.
2. P5 as written demanded that the single-rect **control** also differ from its
   own bounding box — impossible by definition. It now applies to the multi-cell
   shapes, which are the ones making the membership claim.

### Gap closed, gap left

**Closed:** non-convex and disconnected blind sets. The law is membership over a
union of rectangles; the bulk conformance gate now includes union worlds (a third
of its cells get a second blocker) and still shows **0 mismatches** (157 cells at
seed default: 100 / 57; 237 cells at seed 11: 152 / 85).

**Left, and named:** rotated or curved occluders. They need polygon membership,
which is a different visibility model — a real scope boundary, not an oversight.
