# Preregistration — R-P1.64: make the posterior refuse to collapse

Claim `60d8c8af-e65f-4fdf-a482-a659b1077ec2`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

Follows R-P1.63, which found that at the prices where querying pays the true
world is eliminated from the posterior in **12/12** held-out cases: one false
positive on one edge removes it permanently, and the posterior then sits on a
wrong world with mass **1.000** while its own expected utility rises from −1.93
to +3.00. R-P1.63 ended with the instruction that nothing about price is
readable until that collapse is fixed. This card fixes it and re-measures.

## The defect, stated exactly

The filter is hard: after a reading, the posterior is
`{w : w agrees with every reading so far}`. With a symmetric sensor
(`TPR 0.8 / FPR 0.2`) a false positive on one edge is enough, because the truth
world is never re-admitted. The number of queries `k` compounds it: with `k`
independent readings the probability that at least one is wrong is
`1 − (1 − 0.2·π_absent − 0.2·π_present)^k`-ish, and any single wrong reading is
fatal. Nothing about this is a bug in R-P1.63's code; it is what the declared
model does.

## The change, and only this change

The posterior becomes a **soft likelihood** instead of a hard filter:

```
P(reading = present | edge present) = TPR
P(reading = present | edge absent)  = FPR
w ∝ ∏_readings  (TPR if w agrees else FPR)
```

No floor is invented, no parameter is tuned, and the sensor stays exactly
`TPR 0.8 / FPR 0.2` — the same instrument R-P1.61/62/63 used. The only change is
that an improbable world is down-weighted instead of deleted, so a single
misreading costs a factor of 4 and the truth can survive many.

A second, mandatory guard: **normalisation**. If every weight underflows to
zero, the posterior keeps the previous weights (a reading no world explains must
not produce an empty distribution). This is recorded as a counter, not hidden.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the truth survives.** At `c = 0.1`, the truth world's posterior weight
  is non-zero in **12/12** held-out cases (R-P1.63: 0/12). Predicted minimum
  weight over the 12 cases: **> 1e-6**.
- **P2 — confidence stops being 1.000.** At `c = 0.1`, the maximum posterior
  weight on any single world has mean **< 0.75** (R-P1.63: 1.000). The model must
  stop claiming certainty it has not earned.
- **P3 — accuracy improves at the same spend.** Top-3 precision at `c = 0.1`
  rises above R-P1.63's **0.361** (1.25 correct / 3.0 proposed per case) on the
  same queries. Predicted: **0.45**.
- **P4 — the utility conclusion survives, or is retracted.** Net utility at
  `c = 0.1` is still **above** passive's −1.833. If it is not, R-P1.63's
  "a price exists" is retracted rather than repaired.
- **P5 — the structural result is unchanged.** At `c = 1.0` the policy still buys
  **zero** queries. Fixing the model must not make expensive queries look
  worthwhile.

Any prediction may fail; a failure is a result and is kept, and P4/P5 are
written so the card can *lose*.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether a soft posterior fixes elimination, and whether the
  price result survives the fix.
- It **may not** claim a learner, real-data headroom, or that a real instrument
  helps. `E6` stays closed. Nothing here is evidence about any corpus: it is the
  same synthetic generator and the same seeds as R-P1.60–R-P1.63, i.e. a
  reanalysis of one sample.
- It **may not** retune any earlier card. R-P1.63's receipt stays exactly as
  measured; this card measures a different decoder on the same fixture.

## Controls and honesty

- **Same-fixture control:** a hard-filter arm in the same script, so both are
  measured on identical queries and the only difference is the update rule.
- **Null arm:** the R-P1.63 calibrated null (same spend, readings independent of
  truth) must not beat passive. Judged against its own miss rate, not against a
  strict zero — R-P1.63 recorded that "0/12" is impossible for a stochastic arm.
- **Known-outcome check:** at `q = 1.0` the soft posterior must agree with the
  hard one (a perfect sensor makes both deterministic). If it does not, the
  likelihood is wrong.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`, never folded together.
- Every number quoted in the receipt is produced by the committed script; the
  receipt is regenerated from it, never hand-edited.

## Limits

- One symmetric sensor, one predicate, one closure rule, independent local
  readings. A real instrument has correlated errors across edges, which a
  diagonal likelihood does not model; a soft posterior would be *over*confident
  under correlation, and this card does not measure that.
- The weights are exact rational products; no sampling, no approximation.

### The known-outcome check, and the instrument bug it caught in itself

The card required soft and hard to agree at a perfect sensor. The first version
of that check compared the two **policies** — which differ by design (utility
gain vs information gain) — and failed, reporting `UNVERIFIABLE` on a correct
likelihood. A second attempt mutated `SENSOR_TPR` on the script module while
`Posterior.update` reads it through the imported R-P1.63 module, so it tested
0.8/0.2 while claiming 1.0/0.0. Both were instrument bugs, not result bugs.

The check now feeds both decoders one identical reading sequence and compares
posterior **support**: **12/12 agree**. That is what proves the likelihood.

### What this card changes

R-P1.63's conclusion survives: a price exists at which querying beats silence.
The mechanism it identified is fixed — the posterior no longer eliminates the
truth, and no longer sits at mass 1.000 on a wrong world. But the fix buys
**honesty, not accuracy**: on this fixture the soft decoder's proposals are no
better than the hard one's, at a lower spend.

**E6 stays closed.** The remaining gap is that the policy's own proposal
quality is unchanged at 0.417, well under any bar that would justify a learner,
and the reason is now measured: 10 noisy readings do not resolve three edges out
of 21.5 candidates, whichever way the posterior is updated.

### Limits

Unchanged from the card, and one added: the likelihood is diagonal, so it
assumes reading errors are independent across edges. A real instrument's errors
correlate, under which a soft posterior is *over*confident and this card's P2
result would not transfer. Not measured here.
