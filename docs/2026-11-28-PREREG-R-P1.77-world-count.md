# Preregistration — R-P1.77: why active selection resolved 8/8 there and nothing here

Claim `a6f8533c-b57f-4dd5-adde-2aaa1707a9bb`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

E6's note has said the same thing through five cards:

> "the missing ingredient is information the surviving-world counts do not carry."

Cards R-P1.60–69 established that on the **graph-repair** task: passive and active
score the same, probes do not help, and a pool-size effect exists. But this lane
also contains a card where active selection **did** work, decisively:

| card | task | worlds | hypotheses | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-P1.47 | finite law selection | **8** | 2 | signed probe resolves **8/8**; passive, magnitude, shuffled **0/8** |
| R-P1.63–69 | graph repair | **958** at `n = 4` | 3-of-21.5 edges | active ≈ passive; no headroom |

Both are exact Bayesian controllers over surviving worlds with expected-information
selection. One resolves everything in a single query; the other cannot beat
guessing. **Nothing in the lane reconciles them**, and until something does, "no
headroom" is a fact about one fixture with no mechanism attached.

## The hypothesis, stated so it can fail

The two fixtures differ in **the number of surviving worlds a single query must
collapse**, and that number is what decides whether one query can be decisive:

- 8 worlds, one binary reading → at best 4 remain, and the law bit is a **two-way
  partition of all 8**, so one well-chosen reading separates them completely;
- 958 worlds, one reading → ~500 remain, and the three proposed edges are chosen
  from a 21.5-candidate pool, so a reading moves a posterior that is nowhere near
  resolved.

So: **one query resolves a hypothesis when the posterior is small enough that the
query partitions it completely, and contributes nothing when it does not.**

## Design — one sweep over a single dimension

The repair fixture, parameterised only by **world count**. `n ∈ {3, 4}` is the
computable range (R-P1.67 measured 36 and 958 worlds; `n = 2` cannot hold six
edges). To reach the small-posterior regime the fixture needs **fewer candidate
edges**, which is the earlier generator at `SIZE = 3, OBSERVED = 2, MISSING = 1`:
one missing edge, so the proposal decision is "which edge", not "which three".

Cells: `n ∈ {3, 4}` × `SIZE ∈ {3, 6}` × passive vs one probe at `k = 1`. 120 seeds.
The solvent is that both generators exist in the repo and neither needs a new
mechanism.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the anchor holds in both regimes.** At `SIZE = 6` active ≈ passive (the
  R-P1.63–69 reading reproduces), and at `SIZE = 3` active **beats** passive by at
  least 0.10. If the small fixture also shows no difference, the hypothesis is
  wrong and the R-P1.47 contrast is not about world count.
- **P2 — the threshold is a count, not a size.** Resolution happens when the
  surviving-world count after the query is **≤ 4**, and not when it is above. The
  receipt records both counts per cell, so this is checked as a threshold rather
  than a trend.
- **P3 — precision, not just resolution.** The small fixture's active arm beats
  passive on **precision** as well as on hitting the true edge, because with one
  candidate the proposal is the query's own answer.
- **P4 — the control still holds.** A shuffled probe never beats passive at either
  `SIZE`. If it does, the effect is not information.
- **P5 — the arithmetic predicts which cells resolve.** `log2(worlds_before) −
  log2(worlds_after) ≥ log2(candidates_proposed)` marks the resolving cells, and
  that marker must match the measured resolution on every cell.

Any prediction may fail and is kept. **P1 at `SIZE = 3` is load-bearing**: a
negative there would retire this hypothesis and leave the R-P1.47 contrast
unexplained, which is itself worth recording.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** explain the R-P1.47-versus-repair contrast as a world-count threshold,
  or report that it is not one.
- It **may not** open E6. Even a clean threshold is a statement about fixture size,
  not evidence that a **learned** ranker beats the analytic one — the analytic one
  is what resolves.
- It **may not** retune R-P1.47 or any repair card. Their receipts stand.
- It **may not** claim a general law: two generators, one predicate, five cards of
  prior evidence in that direction and one in this.

## Controls and honesty

- Both `SIZE` values run on the **same seeds and the same entity counts**, so the
  only difference is how many edges are missing.
- The shuffled control is per cell, as R-P1.63 established: judged against its own
  miss rate, not against zero.
- `log2` arithmetic is recorded as arithmetic; P5 compares a prediction to a
  measurement, not two measurements to each other.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

Two generators, `n ≤ 4` (the computable range R-P1.67 established), one predicate,
`k = 1`. The small fixture is close to R-P1.47's regime but not identical — R-P1.47
is a scalar law table with no geometry, and this is a graph proposal — so a match
here is a threshold shared across two tasks, not a claim that they are the same
task.
