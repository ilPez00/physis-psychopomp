# PREREG — R-P1.37 containment-encoding loss: can a learned arm be made safe? (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**; claim registered in the store before the
run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.37.

R-P1.36 rejected a learned edge correction, and the reason is precise: it optimised
**mean squared error**, so it bought average accuracy with the conservative tail
(MAE 0.145 → 0.091 while pessimism 0.09 → 0.30). The property a self-model needs is
not accuracy, it is **containment**: never claim to have seen what you did not.

This card tests whether changing the **objective** — not the features, not the
geometry, not the data — makes a learned arm safe. If it does, the R-P1.36 negative
was about the loss; if it does not, learned arms are simply the wrong tool here.

## What changes and what does not

Unchanged: the geometry, the corpora, the features (crossing-bound statistics plus
the known σ), the target (the signed margin `g`), the split, the architecture
(12 → 24 → 1), 400 epochs, seeds, and the held-out σ grid `{0.3, 1.0, 1.5}`.

Changed: the **loss**. Instead of MSE, a **pinball loss at τ = 0.9** on the residual
`r = ĝ − g`:

    L = τ·r        if r ≥ 0      (over-prediction: would make the estimate pessimistic)
    L = (τ−1)·r    if r < 0

Over-prediction costs 9× under-prediction, so the model is pushed to leave the
corrected margin `m = g − ĝ` on the safe side.

Arms: **pinball-learned**, **MSE-learned** (R-P1.36's arm, retrained here for a
like-for-like comparison), **pinball shuffled control**, and the deterministic
inner bound.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **Q1 (the loss is the fix).** At held-out σ = 1.0 the pinball arm's pessimism
  rate is strictly below **both** the deterministic bound's and the MSE arm's.
- **Q2 (it is not a control artifact).** At σ = 1.0 pinball beats its own
  shuffled-target control, and that control is within 0.10 of the deterministic
  baseline.
- **Q3 (it transfers).** At the unseen σ = 1.5 the pinball arm's pessimism is
  strictly below the deterministic bound's.
- **Q4 (the price is bounded and is paid in accuracy, not safety).** Median
  absolute margin is no worse than the deterministic bound's by more than **0.15**
  on any held-out σ — the opposite trade to R-P1.36, which paid in safety.

## Prior

**0.5.** The principled objection to R-P1.36 is answered by construction, but three
things can still sink it: 4000 rows may be too few to learn a 0.9 quantile, the
pinball arm may over-correct so far that it reports systematically *inside* the
truth (which Q4 is designed to detect), or the shuffled control may land near
enough that Q2 fails. I would not be surprised by a partial pass.

## Rules

- No retune. If the pinball arm fails, that is the answer and learner arms stop.
- The loss, τ, architecture, data and split are declared here; nothing widened
  afterwards.
- This is the **second and last** learned attempt in this arc: a failure closes the
  learner line rather than inviting a third loss function.

## Execution status — run 1, 2026-11-22: Q1/Q3 UPHELD, Q2/Q4 FALSIFIED

| held-out σ | deterministic | MSE arm | **pinball arm** | pinball control | det. median | MSE median | **pinball median** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 0.09 | 0.30 | **0.00** | 0.00 | 0.149 | 0.086 | **0.795** |
| 1.0 | 0.53 | 0.61 | **0.00** | 0.41 | 0.176 | 0.174 | **0.616** |
| 1.5 (unseen) | 0.61 | 0.68 | **0.00** | 0.56 | 0.265 | 0.281 | **0.527** |

- **Q1 UPHELD** — the pinball arm reaches **zero pessimism** at σ = 1.0, against
  0.53 for the deterministic bound and 0.61 for the MSE arm.
- **Q2 FALSIFIED, and this is the methodological finding.** The pinball arm beats
  its shuffled control (0.00 vs 0.41), *but the control itself lands 0.12 below the
  deterministic baseline*, outside the 0.10 band. A shuffled-target pinball net
  still learns one thing: **the loss's global bias**. Charging 9× for
  over-prediction shifts every prediction down by roughly the τ-quantile, and a
  constant shift buys most of the "safety" without learning anything per-sample.
  So the pinball arm's advantage is **partly a bias, not a skill**, and this
  control design cannot separate the two.
- **Q3 UPHELD** — zero pessimism carries to the unseen σ = 1.5.
- **Q4 FALSIFIED, and it is the decisive one.** The pinball arm's **median absolute
  error is 0.62–0.80 units** — two to three steps — against the deterministic
  bound's 0.15–0.27. It bought safety by **systematically under-reporting its own
  blind region**, i.e. by claiming vision it does not have. For a self-model, that
  is the failure mode the whole estimator exists to prevent, merely inverted.

### The finding

> A learned arm can be made one-sidedly safe by changing the loss, but **not both
> safe and accurate** at this data scale: MSE spends the conservative tail
> (pessimism 0.61) and pinball spends the honest estimate (median error 0.62–0.80,
> ~2–3 steps), and a shuffled-target control captures ~a third of pinball's
> apparent gain as a global bias. The deterministic inner bound — 0.53 pessimism,
> 0.18 median error — remains the best self-model available here.

That closes the learner line, as this card's rules said it would: two attempts,
two different failures, no third loss function. If a learned self-model is worth
revisiting, it needs a falsifiable way to separate bias from skill (e.g. a
per-sample control matched on prediction variance), which this program has not
built and does not here pretend to have.
