# PREREG — R-P1.15 the noise envelope and its breakpoint (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.15.
R-P1.14 measured the coverage rule surviving σ ≤ 0.5 and left one thing
explicitly unmeasured: **where does it stop?** Its ratio trend was 61.6 → 33.0 →
21.6 → 12.7 at σ = 0.1 → 0.5. This card measures the crossing.

## What the existing data predicts (derived now, before any new run)

From R-P1.14 run 2, per-realization distances:

- `d(true)` ≈ **4.3 σ** (0.43, 0.81, 1.24, 2.15 at σ = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5)
- `d(null)` ≈ **26.5** and essentially σ-independent (26.40 → 27.28)

A two-candidate ranking with independent noise reduces to a comparison of
`d(true) ≈ 4.3σ` against `d(null) ≈ 26.5`. It should stay near-perfect while
`4.3σ ≪ 26.5` and degrade as that ratio approaches 1, i.e. around
**σ ≈ 5–6**, with a knee earlier (accuracy starts to slip when the noise
distance reaches roughly a third of the structural one, σ ≈ 2).

## Design

Same geometry, cells, and metric as R-P1.14 (`o2 = (40.5, 60.5, 35, 65)`;
`uncovered` event at `x = 37.0`, `covered` at `x = 49.0`; candidates
`[true, none]`, independent stream per candidate, 100 realizations per σ with
`episode = 1000·σ_index + r`).

σ ∈ {0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 6.0} — 7 levels, 1400 episodes. Plus the
R-P1.14 anchors at σ ≤ 0.5 are quoted, not re-measured.

Reported per σ: rank accuracy (95% CI), mean `d(true)`, mean `d(null)`, and the
ratio. Breakpoint `σ*` is defined **before** the run as the smallest σ in the
grid whose rank accuracy < 0.90.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (linear noise term).** `d(true)` scales linearly in σ with slope
  **k ∈ [3.5, 5.0]** (fit through the seven points, no intercept forcing beyond
  the σ = 0 anchor of 0).
  *Falsified if* k leaves that band or the fit is non-monotone.
- **P2 (structural term is noise-free).** `d(null)` stays in **[24, 30]** at
  every σ: the structural difference does not scale with sensor noise.
  *Falsified by* any σ outside that band.
- **P3 (the knee).** Rank accuracy is **≥ 0.90 for σ ≤ 2.0** and **< 0.50 for
  σ ≥ 4.0**, and non-increasing across the seven levels.
  *Falsified if* any of those three clauses fails.
- **P4 (breakpoint range).** `σ* ∈ [2.0, 6.0]`. The point prediction from the
  ratio above is **σ ≈ 5–6**; the prereg's band is deliberately wider because
  the crossing is a tail event of a noise distribution, not a deterministic
  threshold.
- **P5 (coverage is untouched by noise).** The covered cell stays within
  **0.5 ± 0.15** at every σ in this grid: noise does not create signal where
  the event is covered.
  *Falsified by* any covered cell outside that band.

## Prior

**0.6.** P1/P2 are extrapolations of a clean linear trend (reasonably strong),
P3's upper clause (≥ 0.90 at σ = 2) is the genuinely open one — `d(true)` at
σ = 2 is ≈ 8.6 against 26.5, about a third, and a third is close enough to hurt.

## Rules

- No retune. If the knee comes earlier than σ = 2, P3 is falsified and the
  envelope is narrower than predicted — which is the useful answer.
- The grid is declared here; no σ added after seeing a knee.

### What the data says instead

The discriminating quantity is the **additive margin** `d(null) − d(true)`,
which is `24.8, 24.1, 23.8, 22.8, 21.5, 20.8, 20.2` — nearly flat, shrinking by
about **0.8 per unit σ**. Extrapolating that line puts the crossing at
**σ ≈ 30**, i.e. a sensor whose ±σ/2 noise is ±15 units on a 2-unit body, in a
100-unit box with 1-unit lanes.

That is a stronger result than the prereg predicted, and it changes the shape of
the claim: the coverage rule is not a fragile signal-detection ordering that
degrades with noise — it is a **paired comparison in which both hypotheses are
corrupted alike**, so the structural difference survives until the noise floor is
comparable to the world's own geometry. My P3 assumed the two distances compete;
they do not, they add.

The crossing itself is **not yet measured**. R-P1.16 (σ ∈ {10, 15, 20, 30, 40},
preregistered from this margin line) tests the σ ≈ 30 extrapolation.
