# Plan — R-P1.9 multi-observer occlusion (2026-11-22, predictions before the run)

Status: **PLAN**, written before the run. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.9.
Successor to R-P1.8: attribution's one irreducible ambiguity was a kick on a
body the camera cannot see. This card asks the mission-P1 question behind it:
**is that limit a property of the world or of the observer set?**

## Change to the instrument

`SimConfig.o2` — a second observer's own occluder. A body is observable when
**at least one** observer sees it, so the blind region is `o ∩ o2`; `None`
reproduces the single-observer instrument exactly (default, all existing gates
unchanged). Two cameras with different occluding geometry are the physical
reading: a second vantage point, not a magic sensor.

No new event kind, no new schema: the second observer only changes which steps
are visible, so the observable log stays `psychopomp.observation/1`.

## Predictions (fixed now, before any run)

- **P1 (reproduction)** — single observer, ball inside the occluder at kick
  time: the simulator skips the kick, the counterfactual equals no-kick, and
  attribution reports ambiguity of size 2 (R-P1.8's limit reproduced).
- **P2 (the claim)** — a second observer whose blind spot is **disjoint** from
  the first makes that kick visible, so it is applied and the truth wins
  uniquely (ambiguity size 1, margin > 0).
- **P3 (the control)** — a second observer with the **same** blind spot
  (`o2 = o`) changes nothing: identical ambiguity to single. What matters is
  coverage overlap, not observer count. A positive that also fires here is an
  artifact.
- **P4 (monotone information)** — crossings that are unobservable (hidden from
  every observer) fall monotonically as blind coverage shrinks:
  `single = same > overlap > disjoint (= 0)`.
- **P5 (no side effects)** — with no interventions, a disjoint second observer
  produces an **identical** observable log to the single observer (same events,
  same values, same order): visibility is unchanged where it was already
  visible, so physics and provenance-carrying content are untouched.

If P2 holds but P3 also holds, the effect is observer count, not information —
report it as such and do not claim the identifiability result.

## Instrument

- `psychopomp/sim.py`: `o2` field, `inside_rect`, `observable(cfg, x, y)`,
  visibility uses `observable`; `Init` guard updated to the same predicate.
- `scripts/multi-observer-bench.py`: four cells (`single`, `same`, `overlap`,
  `disjoint`), per cell: hidden crossing count, hidden step fraction,
  attribution over `[true, none, hidden-time]`, ambiguity size, margin.

Registered claim: **none** (instrument card). Receipt:
`research/loops/results/multi-observer-20261122/receipt.json`.

## Limits, stated now

- Two observers are modelled as two visibility masks, not two rendered views;
  no parallax, no perspective distortion, no per-observer noise. R-P1.2/P1.3
  cover frames and degradation separately.
- The blind region is a rectangle intersection; non-convex occluders are out of
  scope.
- Attribution stays noise-free and finite-hypothesis.

## Run 1 (2026-11-22) — executed, P2 and P5 FALSIFIED, instrument at fault

| cell | blind | hidden step frac | visible kick | hidden kick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single | o | 0.331 | unique, d(null)=16.05 | **tie with null** (ambiguity 2) |
| same | o | 0.331 | unique, d(null)=16.05 | **tie with null** (ambiguity 2) |
| overlap | o∩o2 | 0.164 | unique | unique, d(null)=12.00 |
| disjoint | ∅ | 0.000 | **ambiguity 3, d(null)=0** | **ambiguity 3, d(null)=0** |

P1, P3, P4 upheld; **P2 falsified** (disjoint was *less* identifiable, not
more) and **P5 falsified** (the two logs differ — 4 events vs 0).

Diagnosis, both are instrument defects, not world facts:

1. **The signature read only occlusion crossings.** A second observer with a
   disjoint occluder removes occlusion entirely, so the observable log has no
   crossings, the signature is empty, and *every* hypothesis scores 0. The
   measurement could not have detected the effect it was testing.
2. **P5 was mis-stated.** "Identical observable log" is impossible by
   construction: the second observer is *supposed* to reveal what the first
   could not. The honest invariant is that observation changes nothing
   physical (identical trajectories) and can only ever reveal (events are a
   subset), never invent.

Also found and fixed, from the crash in this run: `_enter_side`/`_exit_side`
were labelled against occluder `o` always, so a crossing caused by `o2` raised
`corner entry is not supported`. They now take the rect that actually hid the
body (regression test pinned).

## Run 2 (2026-11-22) — after the fix, all five UPHELD

Signature vocabulary extended to every observable kind (`kick`, `enter_occ`,
`exit_occ`, `wall_bounce`); P5 restated as trajectory invariance + reveal-only.
R-P1.8's bench re-run unchanged: 12/12 unique top-1 both families, control
0/12 (margins rise to 7.94 → 30.57 and 17.20 → 30.57 because kick events now
contribute distance).

| cell | blind | hidden step frac | visible kick | hidden kick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single | o | 0.331 | unique, margin 26.05 | **tie with null** (ambiguity 2) |
| same | o | 0.331 | unique, margin 26.05 | **tie with null** (ambiguity 2) |
| overlap | o∩o2 | 0.164 | unique, margin 31.00 | **unique**, margin 22.00 |
| disjoint | ∅ | 0.000 | unique, margin 10.00 | **unique**, margin 10.00 |

- **P1 UPHELD** — one observer: a kick while the body is inside the occluder is
  skipped, `d(true) = d(null) = 0.00`, ambiguity size 2. R-P1.8's limit
  reproduced exactly.
- **P2 UPHELD** — a second observer with a *different* blind spot makes the
  same kick visible, so it is applied and the truth wins uniquely with margin
  22.00 (overlap) / 10.00 (disjoint).
- **P3 UPHELD** — a second observer with the *same* blind spot is
  indistinguishable from one observer (identical skip list, identical log,
  identical ambiguity). Coverage overlap, not observer count, is the variable.
- **P4 UPHELD** — hidden step fraction 0.331 = 0.331 > 0.164 > 0.000.
- **P5 UPHELD** — trajectories byte-identical under a second observer, and the
  disjoint log is a subset (0 ⊆ 4 events) of the single-observer log.

## What this is and is not

Measured: **an identifiability limit is a property of the observer set, not of
the world.** The same episode, the same physics and the same hypotheses go from
"cannot tell a kick from no kick" to "unique, margin 10–22" purely by adding a
second vantage point with a different blind spot — and nothing changes when the
second vantage point shares the first's blind spot.

Not measured: parallax, per-observer noise, non-rectangular occluders, more
than two observers, or any learned arm. Attribution is still noise-free and
finite-hypothesis; the `track`/`pos` channels are unchanged.
