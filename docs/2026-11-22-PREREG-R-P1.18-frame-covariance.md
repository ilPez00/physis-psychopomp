# PREREG — R-P1.18 is identifiability frame-covariant? (2026-11-22)

Status: **registered before the run**. Roadmap `docs/ROADMAP.md` R-P1.18.
A4 (2026-11-21) falsified "perspective-normalized analysis beats world-frame
analysis": a frame change is a bijection on the observables, so it cannot add a
decision. That result left the *right* frame question unasked. This card asks it:

> Is the coverage law **frame-covariant** — unchanged when the whole scene,
> observers included, is transformed — and is that different from transforming
> the bodies alone (which is what A4 did)?

If the law is covariant, then identifiability is a property of the world *and*
its observers together, and A4's negative is explained rather than merely
recorded: comparing frames that move the bodies but not the observers compares
different worlds.

## Instruments

- `psychopomp/perspective.py`: `scene_symmetry(name)` → exact symmetries of the
  square box, applied to **positions, velocities, and both occluders**:
  `identity`, `rot90`, `rot180`, `reflect-v`, `reflect-h`.
- `scripts/frame-covariance-prereg.py`: 3 event positions × 2 observer drifts ×
  5 symmetries = 30 cells, plus a bodies-only control.

All five maps send the 100 × 100 box to itself and rectangles to rectangles, so
no physics needs re-deriving: constant velocity, elastic walls and rectangular
occluders are exactly equivariant under them. A non-square box or a 45° rotation
would break that, and is **out of scope** by construction, not by omission.

## Grid

Cells from R-P1.17 (same world): events `x = 37, 44, 55` at `t = 60`,
`y = 45`; observer `static` or drifting `right` (`o2_velocity = (1.667, 0)`).
Predictions for the identity frame are R-P1.17's measured ones.

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **P1 (covariance).** For every cell, the tie/unique outcome is **identical**
  in all five symmetries. 30/30. *One mismatch falsifies.*
- **P2 (the map is exact).** Blind-region area is preserved by every symmetry
  (600 → 600 etc.) and the mapped blind region is exactly the image of the
  original — checked as `blind(sym(world)) == sym(blind(world))`.
  *Falsified by* any area or image mismatch.
- **P3 (the control fails, and that is the point).** If the **bodies** are
  transformed but the occluders are held fixed — A4's implicit comparison —
  at least one cell's identifiability changes. If nothing changes, the control
  is not sensitive and P1's covariance claim is untested by this design.
- **P4 (numeric covariance).** In every frame `d(true) = 0.00` and `d(null)` is
  the same value up to floating-point (the structural difference is invariant),
  so the *decision margins* are covariant too, not just the labels.

## Prior

**0.85** for P1/P2/P4 (exact geometry, nothing stochastic). **0.8** for P3: the
control should flip a cell, but if the events happen to be placed where the
transform maps them to equivalent coverage, it might not — in which case P3 is
falsified and the design is weaker than claimed.

## Rules

- No retune. A mismatch is a receipt.
- Symmetries are exact and enumerated here; no continuous rotation sweep (that
  would require a non-axis-aligned box, a different instrument).

## Execution status — run 1, 2026-11-22: all four UPHELD

- **P1 UPHELD** — 30/30 cells keep their outcome (tie or unique) under all five
  symmetries. Examples from the drift table: `right/E44` is unique in
  `identity`, `rot90`, `rot180`, `reflect-v`, `reflect-h` with `d(null) = 28.30`
  in every one; `right/E55` is a tie in all five with `d(null) = 0.00`.
- **P2 UPHELD** — the blind region maps exactly under every symmetry
  (`blind(sym(world)) == sym(blind(world))`, tolerance 1e-9) and its area is
  preserved exactly. `rot90` maps `[40.5, 60] × [35, 65]` to
  `[35, 65] × [40, 59.498]` — same area, rotated.
- **P3 UPHELD** — the bodies-only control is **sensitive**: 22 buildable cells,
  **5 flips**, and 2 cells that cannot even be built because the transformed body
  starts inside an occluder (i.e. the "transformed" world is a different world).
  Examples: `rot90 static E37` unique → tie; `rot180 right E55` tie → unique.
- **P4 UPHELD** — `d(true) = 0.00` in all 30 cells and `d(null)` is identical
  across frames to 1e-6 (28.30, 10.00, 0.00 by cell). The *decision margins* are
  covariant, not merely the labels.

### What this settles, and how it changes A4

> Identifiability is **frame-covariant**: it is unchanged by any exact symmetry
> of the world that carries the observers with it (translation, 90°/180°
> rotation, reflection of an axis-aligned scene), because coverage is a
> geometric predicate on a transformed pair (event, occluders).

And A4's negative is now *explained* rather than merely recorded: transforming
the **bodies** while holding the observers fixed changes which world is being
observed (5 of 22 cells flip, 2 more become unbuildable). Those frames are not
perspectives on one world — they are different worlds. So "perspective
normalization adds no predictive value" (A4, H1 falsified) is the correct answer
to the *wrong* invariance question: A4 compared worlds, not viewpoints.

The two results are compatible and now jointly state the mission's frame claim:

- **viewpoint changes that move the observers with the scene: no effect**
  (frames cannot create information — A4, and P1 here);
- **changes that move only the observed bodies: large effect** (they are world
  changes — P3 here).
