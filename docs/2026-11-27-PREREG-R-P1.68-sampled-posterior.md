# Preregistration — R-P1.68: sampling the world set, so the realistic pools are measurable

Claim `8c78c316-25d9-428b-83df-7aafeac41b87`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

R-P1.67 measured the pool trend at `n ∈ {3, 4}` and named its own limit:

> "`n = 6/8` … are **unmeasured because it is not computable** with exhaustive
> enumeration. Sampling the world set, or a posterior that does not enumerate, is
> the follow-up that would make those cells reachable; this card does not claim
> them."

This is that follow-up. It is a **method card first**: the question is whether a
sampled posterior reproduces the exhaustive one where both are computable, and
only then whether it reaches the pools an operator's graph actually has.

## Why enumeration is the cost, and sampling is not

Measured in R-P1.67: at `n = 6` a case has 63 candidate edges and **16,706**
surviving worlds; at `n = 8`, 117 and **222,130**. The per-query cost is
`O(worlds × pool)` — every candidate edge's marginal is a sum over every world —
so `n = 8` is ~13× the work of `n = 6` per query, compounded by `k` queries and
the information-gain loop.

Sampling the surviving set is cheap by comparison: a candidate world is 3 edges
from the unobserved pool, and the cap rule rejects only a share of them (measured:
~49% at `n = 6`, ~92% at `n = 8`), so uniform rejection sampling needs a handful
of draws per accepted world and **replaces 222,130 worlds with M**.

## Design

The R-P1.67 fixture, unchanged in every respect except the posterior's support:

- soft likelihood (R-P1.64), sensor `TPR 0.8 / FPR 0.2`, `k ∈ {4, 8, 12}`;
- truth and observations drawn exactly as before, from the same seeds;
- the surviving set is **sampled** uniformly with `M ∈ {500, 2000, 8000}`, seeded
  per case so the draw is reproducible;
- the same information-gain query order, the same top-3 proposal, the same
  metrics (`precision`, `completion_error`, `mean_surviving`).

Cells: `n ∈ {3, 4, 6, 8}` × `k ∈ {4, 8, 12}` × `M ∈ {500, 2000, 8000}`, plus the
exhaustive arm wherever it is computable (`n = 3, 4`).

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the sampler is faithful.** At `n = 3` and `n = 4`, precision under
  `M = 8000` is within **±0.10** of the exhaustive arm at every `k`. A larger gap
  means the sampler is biased (it would be a defect, not a result).
- **P2 — 2000 samples suffice.** At `n = 4`, `M = 2000` is within ±0.10 of
  exhaustive; below that the card reports the required M rather than pretending.
- **P3 — the realistic pools become measurable.** `n = 6` and `n = 8` produce
  precision and completion error in bounded time, and the `n = 6` cell's
  precision is **below** the `n = 4` cell's at the same `k` — the R-P1.67 trend
  continues to the pools an operator actually has.
- **P4 — the cost is bounded by M, not by the world count.** Wall time per case
  at `n = 8`, `M = 2000` is within **4×** of `n = 4`, `M = 2000`. If time still
  scales with the enumerated world count, sampling has not removed the cost.
- **P5 — no leak from sampling.** The null arm (same spend, readings independent
  of truth, sampled support) does not beat passive at any `n`, judged against its
  own miss rate.

Any prediction may fail and is kept. **P1 failing is the important one**: it
would mean this card cannot substitute for enumeration, and the realistic pools
stay unmeasured.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether sampling reproduces exhaustive posteriors, and give the
  precision trend for pools of 63 and 117.
- It **may not** claim the sampled numbers are exact. Sampling is an
  approximation with a declared `M`, and every cell reports it.
- It **may not** open E6. A trend in precision across pool sizes is a statement
  about the task; E5/R-P1.60's headroom question stays where it was.
- It **may not** retune R-P1.67's cells, thresholds or receipt. That card's
  `n = 3, 4` numbers stand as measured.

## Controls and honesty

- Exhaustive arms at `n = 3, 4` are the ground truth for P1/P2 — computed by the
  same code path with `M = None`, never by a different implementation.
- Three `M` values, so a sampling artefact shows up as a trend in `M` rather than
  being read as an effect of pool size.
- The receipt records, per cell, the wall time, the accepted-draw fraction and
  the number of worlds the sampler saw, so P4 is checkable.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

Uniform rejection sampling over 3-edge completions; a pool where the cap rule
rejected most candidates would make this expensive and is not the case here
(measured acceptance is high at these `n`). One predicate, one cap rule, one
sensor, one decoder. Sampling error is bounded only for the `M` measured — no
claim about `M` much smaller than 500.
