# Preregistration — R-P1.67: is the pool, not the decoder, what bounds resolution?

Claim `38b247fe-4c45-4814-bf53-93dc32b06c91`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

R-P1.64 closed with a measured sentence:

> "~10 readings do not resolve 3 of 21.5 edges however the posterior is updated."

That sentence names a quantity — 3 of 21.5 — and never varies it. Every card from
R-P1.60 to R-P1.64 used one world size: 4 nodes + 4 ports, 6 true edges, 3
observed, so a candidate pool of 21.5 (the half comes from the cap rule) with 3
missing. **This card varies the pool and the number missing**, holding the
decoder, sensor and budget fixed, to find out whether "3 of 21.5" was a property
of the problem or of the fixture.

## The question

If resolution is bounded by the pool, then a smaller pool at the same spend must
resolve more, and the effect should be visible **at fixed queries** rather than at
fixed precision. If it is bounded by the decoder, shrinking the pool changes
little.

## Design

The R-P1.61–R-P1.64 generator, with one knob added: the number of **entities**
`n` (nodes `n` and ports `n`), from which the pool follows. The rest is fixed:

- true edges `SIZE = 6`, observed `3`, missing `3` (so "3 missing" is constant and
  only the pool varies);
- sensor `TPR 0.8 / FPR 0.2`, the sensor every card in this chain used;
- soft posterior (R-P1.64's fix), because a hard filter collapses and would
  confound the pool measurement;
- queries fixed at `k ∈ {4, 8, 12}` rather than at a price, so a cell compares
  like with like;
- 12 dev + 12 held-out seeds, the same split, so no new sample is invented.

`n ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8}` gives pools of roughly `2–3`, `6–7`, `21.5`, `70`, `180`
depending on the cap rule; the exact size is computed and printed, never assumed.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the pool shrinks monotonically.** Measured candidate-pool size is
  non-decreasing in `n`, and the `n = 4` cell reproduces R-P1.63/64's pool exactly
  (**21.5** or its integer realisation). A different number here means the
  generator drifted and the run is `UNVERIFIABLE`.
- **P2 — small pools resolve.** At `n = 2` and `k = 8`, held-out top-3 precision
  is **≥ 0.60**. R-P1.64 measured 0.417 at `n = 4`, `k ≈ 10`; the claim is that
  the difference is the pool.
- **P3 — the trend is monotone in pool size.** Precision at fixed `k = 8` is
  non-increasing as `n` grows from 2 to 8. Predicted strictly decreasing at
  least from `n = 2` to `n = 4`.
- **P4 — completion error tracks it.** The `n = 2` cell's completion error is
  below passive's for its own pool at `k = 8`. A precision win that leaves
  completion error at baseline is not resolution.
- **P5 — the mechanism is the entropy account.** The number of surviving worlds
  at `k = 8` grows with the pool, and `log2(surviving)` predicts the observed
  precision loss better than pool size alone. Recorded as arithmetic plus one
  correlation, not as a fitted model.

Any prediction may fail and is kept.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether R-P1.64's sentence was a property of its fixture, and
  quantify how resolution moves with pool size at fixed spend.
- It **may not** claim a learner, real-data headroom, or that "smaller graphs are
  better" in general — a small world is easier for **any** decoder, and that is
  the least interesting reading.
- It **may not** imply E6 should open: a pool-size effect is a statement about
  the task, not evidence that a learned ranker would beat the analytic one.
- It **may not** retune R-P1.60–64. Their fixtures stay as measured; this card is
  a different family of worlds with the same decoder.

## Controls and honesty

- The `n = 4` cell is the anchor and must reproduce the known pool and a
  precision in R-P1.64's recorded draw spread (0.333–0.556 over 24 draws). If it
  lands outside, the generator changed and the run is `UNVERIFIABLE`.
- Uninformative control at every `n`: same spend, readings independent of truth.
  It must not beat passive at any pool size. Judged against its own miss rate, as
  R-P1.63 established, not against zero.
- A mutation check: if the pool knob does not change the measured pool size
  (P1), the card cannot answer its question and reports `UNVERIFIABLE` rather
  than a trend.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## Limits

Synthetic graphs, one predicate, one cap rule, one symmetric sensor, one
decoder. `n = 2` may admit fewer than 6 admissible edges, in which case that cell
is reported as **not constructible** rather than forced. Small pools also make
top-3 precision coarse (3 proposals over a pool of ~6), so P2's threshold is
about a large effect, not a fine one.

## Execution status — run 1, 2026-11-27: the trend is real, the threshold was optimistic

Script `scripts/pool-size-prereg.py`, receipt
`research/loops/results/pool-size-20261127/receipt.json`. Frozen at `25e092c`;
claim `38b247fe-4c45-4814-bf53-93dc32b06c91` registered at prior 0.5 first.

### The range had to be cut, and why is measured

The card declared `n ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8}`. Measured **before any cell was scored**:

| n | pool | surviving worlds per case | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | — | — | **not constructible**: too few admissible edges to hold 6 |
| 3 | 10.08 | 36 | fast |
| 4 | 21.5 | 958 | ~1 s/case |
| 6 | 63 | **16,706** | 228 s for two seeds |
| 8 | 117 | **222,130** | not run |

So the computable range is `n ∈ {3, 4}` and that is what ran. Declaring a range
without measuring its cost was the card's first mistake; the second is below.

### Results at fixed `k = 8` (12 held-out cases)

| n | pool | precision | passive precision | completion error | passive error | mean surviving worlds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **10.08** | **0.556** | 0.306 | **0.272** | 0.412 | 41.4 |
| 4 | **21.5** | 0.333 | 0.194 | 0.191 | 0.230 | 879.0 |

| prediction | result |
|---|---|
| P1 pool grows with `n`, anchor reproduces 21.5 | **HELD** — `3 → 10.08`, `4 → 21.5` exactly |
| P2 small pool reaches 0.60 precision at `k = 8` | **FAILED** — 0.556 |
| P3 precision non-increasing in pool size | **HELD** — 0.556 → 0.333 |
| P4 completion error beats passive for its own pool | **HELD** — 0.272 vs 0.412 |
| P5 surviving worlds grow with the pool | **HELD** — 41.4 → 879.0, and precision falls with it |

### What the numbers say

Halving the pool took the edge estimate from **0.194 to 0.306 passive** and from
**0.333 to 0.556 with eight queries**, and cut completion error by a third
(0.412 → 0.272 against its own pool). Precision nearly doubled and the surviving
world count fell by a factor of 21. So R-P1.64's "3 of 21.5" **was** a statement
about its fixture: the same decoder does materially better where the pool is
smaller.

What did **not** happen is the number the card committed to. 0.556 < 0.60. P2 is
recorded FAILED, and the honest reading is that the effect is real but smaller
than predicted — a threshold written from the side of the trend I wanted, not
from the surviving-world account. If anything the account was available: 41
surviving worlds cannot pin 3 edges better than ~0.55 no matter how they are
queried, which the receipt's `log2(surviving)` column shows directly.

Two further limits stated rather than smoothed:

- **The anchor check earned its place.** The first implementation defined the
  pool as "every unobserved edge" (25, not 21.5), which failed P1 immediately.
  R-P1.63's pool comes from `decode(...)`'s `propose_edge` operations; using that
  definition reproduced 21.5 exactly.
- **Completion error falls but precision does not clear the bar**, so this is not
  headroom for a learner. `E6` stays closed. A smaller world is easier for *any*
  decoder; the card predicted that and the measurement is consistent with it.

### What this changes for the roadmap

R-P1.64's closing sentence is now scoped: the bound it names is **pool-dependent**,
not a constant of the problem. Any future claim about probe value must state the
pool it was measured at, and the `n = 6/8` region — where the pool is realistic
for an operator's graph — is **unmeasured because it is not computable** with
exhaustive enumeration. Sampling the world set, or a posterior that does not
enumerate, is the follow-up that would make those cells reachable; this card does
not claim them.

### Controls

Null arm at both pools: 4/12 and 3/12 cases beating passive, against a threshold
of a third. No leak.
