# Preregistration — R-P1.17: time-resolved coverage

Claim `2c2d23e3-3752-4c8f-a09d-54b2053f1ef7`, registered at **prior 0.5** before the run. Freeze on origin before
any measurement. Status at freeze: **not measured**.

The roadmap's last genuinely `planned` item. R-P1.10 measured that identifiability
follows **coverage at the event step**, not aggregate blind area. Every geometry
tested there held the occluders still. This card makes observer 2's blind region
move in time and asks whether the rule survives a moving frame.

## The question, stated as a prediction that can fail

If identifiability is "was the event outside the blind region *at the event
step*", then a moving occluder should change the answer **only** by moving that
region across the event location at that step. Two consequences are testable and
one is a trap:

- A kick hidden by the static occluder must become identifiable when the drift
  carries the blind region away from it — and must stay hidden when it carries
  it *across* the kick.
- The same event, under the same drift, must flip class depending only on
  whether it is inside `o ∩ o2(t_event)`.
- **Trap:** the counterfactual must hold the observer fixed. If a candidate
  episode re-integrates the occluder with a different step count, the drift
  differs between observed and counterfactual and any "identification" is an
  artefact of the observer moving differently, not of the world.

## Fixture

R-P1.9's geometry (`INIT`, `HIDDEN_KICK` at t=60, `VISIBLE_KICK` at t=30), whose
static behaviour is already pinned by `tests/test_multi_observer.py`. Observer 2
exists in two positions:

- `BLIND_AT_KICK = (50, 70, 35, 65)` — covers the hidden kick's location
  `(47.0, 30.5)`, hence R-P1.20/R-P1.23's known geometry.
- `CLEAR_AT_KICK` — 40 units away in y, so the kick is outside it at t=60.

Drifts: `static (0,0)`, `up (0,-1)`, `down (0,+1)` world units per step, and one
`across (+1,0)`. 2 event locations × 4 drifts = **8 cells**, each with 5 seeds
(0–4). Blind region at the event step computed from `occluder2_at`, not assumed.

## Frozen predictions

- **P1 — the rule holds under drift.** For every cell, `unique` identification
  occurs **iff** the event location is outside `o ∩ o2(t_event)`. Predicted:
  **8/8 cells** agree, and the agreement is exact rather than aggregate.
- **P2 — a static blind region hides regardless of seeds.** Where the event is
  inside the blind region at the event step, the result ties with the null
  (ambiguity 2) on **5/5 seeds**; it never "sometimes" identifies.
- **P3 — drift can un-hide, and that is not headroom.** At least one cell must
  change class between `static` and a drifting arm **for the same event**, and
  the change must be predicted from the blind region at `t_event` before the
  run. Predicted: the `up` arm on `CLEAR_AT_KICK` flips hidden→unique.
- **P4 — the trap: a drifting observer must not leak resolution.** For a cell
  where the event is inside the blind region at the event step under every
  drift, the counterfactual observer must move identically, and the result must
  stay tied with the null. If a drifting cell "identifies" an event that its own
  event-step blind region covers, the observation is contaminated and the run is
  **UNVERIFIABLE**, not a win.
- **P5 — permutations stay at chance.** A permuted-score control must not beat
  the null in any cell (measured against its own rate, never a strict zero).

Any prediction may fail; a failure is a result and is kept.

## What this card may and may not conclude

- It **may** say whether R-P1.10's coverage rule survives a time-varying blind
  region, and name the cells where it does not.
- It **may not** claim a general theory of moving observers: one geometry, one
  drift per axis, four velocities, synthetic. Nothing transfers without
  re-measuring the geometry.
- It **may not** claim headroom for a learned arm, and it may not touch
  R-P1.10's or R-P1.23's receipts.
- If P1 fails, the honest conclusion is that the coverage rule is a **snapshot
  rule** — true only for static observers — and R-P1.10's claim needs that scope
  written into it.

## Honesty requirements

- Every cell's blind region is computed by `occluder2_at(cfg, t_event)` and
  printed in the receipt, so the prediction is checkable rather than narrated.
- The observer is held fixed across the observed episode and every
  counterfactual; the attribution path already re-runs the same config, and the
  receipt records the step count used for each.
- Verdict vocabulary: `PASS` / `FAIL` / `UNVERIFIABLE`; P4 can force the last.
- Negative control: shuffled hypothesis labels, which must not identify.

## Limits

- The occluder reflects at the world edge (`_fold`), so a drift long enough
  wraps. Cells are chosen so the wrap does not occur before the event step; the
  receipt records the occluder's position at every step to prove it.
- One ball pair, one kick magnitude, rigid rectangular occluders only.
